15. Producer registration and extended producer responsibility ​

Producer registration and extended producer responsibility (EPR) apply to every battery category, including batteries incorporated in appliances, light means of transport or other vehicles. They are not limited to batteries that require a battery passport. The decisive questions are who first makes the battery available in a Member State, how it is supplied and whether an authorised representative for extended producer responsibility or a producer responsibility organisation acts for that producer (Article 3(1), points (47)–(49); Articles 55–58).

15.1 Meaning of producer ​

“Producer” is a role determined separately for the market of each Member State; it is not necessarily the manufacturer named on the battery. Article 3(1), point (47), covers a person that, irrespective of selling technique: • is established in a Member State and first supplies there batteries manufactured under its own name or trademark; • is established in a Member State and first resells there, under its own name or trademark, batteries made by another party whose name or trademark does not appear; • is established in a Member State and first supplies there, on a professional basis, batteries from another Member State or a third country; or • is established in another Member State or a third country and sells batteries by distance contract directly to end-users in the Member State concerned.

Each limb includes batteries incorporated in appliances, light means of transport or other vehicles. A party can consequently be the producer in one Member State without being the producer for the same battery in another. Manufacturer, importer and distributor roles under Chapter VI and the producer role under Chapter VIII must be assessed independently.

An economic operator that first makes available in a Member State a battery resulting from preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing is treated as the producer of that battery and assumes EPR (Article 56(2)). Further details on obligations following circular operations are provided in Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”.

15.2 Member State determination ​

The producer determination and its evidence should be maintained for each combination of legal entity, Member State, supply route and battery category.

QuestionRegulatory significance
In which Member State is the battery first made available?Determines the register, competent authority and EPR system (Articles 55(2) and 56(1)).
Which party performs the relevant first supply or direct distance sale?Determines the producer under Article 3(1), point (47).
Is the battery incorporated in another product?It remains within registration and reporting (Articles 55(2) and 75(4)).
What is the battery category and chemistry?These affect registration, reporting, collection and contribution modulation (Articles 55(3), 57(2) and 75).
Has the battery undergone a listed circular operation?The party first marketing the resulting battery becomes a producer (Article 56(2)).
Is it a direct distance sale from outside the Member State?The seller is a producer and must appoint an EPR authorised representative there (Articles 3(1), point (47)(d), and 56(3)).

Where a battery, identified by a GTIN, is incorporated in or supplied with another trade item, the battery category, chemistry and quantity describe the battery, while the host-product GTIN and description identify the appliance, light means of transport or vehicle. The two objects must remain distinguishable and linked.

15.3 Producer registration ​

Each Member State must establish a producer register. A producer must apply electronically in every Member State where it first makes a battery available and may supply there only if it—or, where applicable, its EPR authorised representative—is registered (Article 55(1)–(2)). Before making a battery available, a distributor must verify that the producer is registered (Article 42(2), point (a)).

The application includes the producer’s name, available brand names, address and contact details; its national identification code, trade-register or equivalent official registration number and tax identification number; every relevant battery category and chemistry; information on the applicable EPR, collection and data-reliability arrangements and any appointed producer responsibility organisation; and a statement that the supplied information is true (Article 55(3)).

Information about EPR and collection arrangements may instead be supplied through the Article 58 authorisation application. Member States may combine registration and authorisation, request additional information or documents necessary to operate the register, establish procedural modalities and charge proportionate cost-based fees.

The producer, EPR authorised representative or appointed producer responsibility organisation, as applicable, must notify changes to registered information and permanent cessation without undue delay (Article 55(12)). Registration data therefore need an owner and a change-control process.

15.4 Authority-issued registration numbers ​

Once a complete application has been provided, the competent authority grants registration and provides a registration number within no more than 12 weeks (Article 55(9), point (b)). This authority-issued identifier must not be replaced by a GTIN, GLN, company registration number, tax number or other commercial identifier.

An implementation stores the official number together with the issuing Member State, competent authority, registered legal entity, covered battery categories, status and effective dates. The Member State is essential because one producer may hold several national registration numbers. If registration information is not public, Member States must give qualifying online-platform providers free access to it (Article 55(13)).

The authority may refuse or withdraw registration where required information or evidence is absent or insufficient or the producer no longer meets the applicable EPR-arrangement requirements; it must withdraw registration where the producer has ceased to exist (Article 55(11)). Systems should therefore validate current status rather than treat possession of a number as permanent proof of compliance.

15.5 Producer responsibility organisations ​

A producer may appoint an authorised producer responsibility organisation (PRO) to fulfil EPR obligations on its behalf, and a Member State may make appointments mandatory, where justified by a battery category’s characteristics and waste management (Article 57(1)). Registration duties may be performed by the PRO mutatis mutandis unless the Member State provides otherwise (Article 55(6)).

For collective fulfilment, a PRO must treat producers equally regardless of origin or size and avoid disproportionate burdens on producers placing small quantities on the market. Contributions must be modulated at least by category and chemistry and adjusted for relevant revenues; modulation also takes account, as appropriate, of rechargeability, recycled content, circular operations and carbon footprint (Article 57(2)). PROs must protect producer-attributable and proprietary information, publish specified performance information at least annually, and publish information about their waste-operator selection procedure (Article 57(4)–(6), (8)).

A producer fulfilling EPR individually, or a PRO fulfilling it collectively, must obtain authorisation and provide the required guarantee (Article 58(1)–(2), (7)). Self-control must occur regularly, at least every three years, and on authority request. Changes and cessation must be notified without undue delay. Failure to maintain collection, reporting or other authorisation conditions can lead to revocation (Article 58(3)–(6)).

15.6 Financing obligations ​

Article 56 assigns producers EPR for batteries they first make available in a Member State. Financial contributions cover:

Cost areaRegulatory source
Separate collection and subsequent transport and treatment, accounting for specified revenuesArticle 56(4), point (a)
Compositional surveys of collected mixed municipal wasteArticle 56(4), point (b), and Article 69(5)
Information on prevention and management of waste batteriesArticle 56(4), point (c), and Article 74

These costs must be shown separately to the end-user at the point of sale of a new battery (Article 74(5)).

For a battery subjected to preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing, the original and subsequent producers may establish a cost-sharing mechanism based on actual attribution for the costs in Article 56(4), points (a), (c) and (d). The first producer must not bear additional costs where the battery is subject to more than one EPR (Article 56(5)). The Regulation permits this mechanism but does not prescribe its technical allocation method. Further details on category-specific collection and treatment duties are provided in Section 16, “Waste collection, treatment and recycling”.

15.7 Distributor take-back ​

Distributors must take back qualifying waste batteries free of charge and without requiring a new purchase. Portable batteries are taken back at or in the immediate vicinity of the retail outlet; LMT, SLI, industrial and EV batteries are taken back at or in its vicinity. The duty is limited to categories that the distributor offers or previously offered and, for portable batteries, to quantities normally discarded by non-professional end-users. It does not apply to waste products containing batteries (Article 62(1)–(2)).

The distributor hands the batteries to the responsible producer or PRO or to a waste management operator selected under Article 57(8) for treatment under Article 70 (Article 62(3)). Handover records should preserve the category, collection point, date and quantity needed by the reporting chain, but Article 62 prescribes neither a GS1 identifier nor a fixed retention period.

Distance-selling distributors must provide sufficient collection points across the Member State. For sales with delivery, distributors must offer free take-back at delivery or a local collection point and inform the end-user about arrangements when ordering (Article 62(4)–(5)). Further details on collection systems are provided in Section 16.1, “Category-specific collection systems”, and on information for end-users in Section 16.3, “End-user information”.

15.8 Online sales and platforms ​

A producer selling directly by distance contract to end-users in a Member State from another Member State or a third country must appoint, by written mandate, an EPR authorised representative in every Member State of sale (Article 3(1), points (47)(d) and (48); Article 56(3)). This includes batteries incorporated in appliances, light means of transport or other vehicles.

For online platforms within Section 4 of Chapter III of Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (the Digital Services Act, DSA) that allow consumers to conclude distance contracts with producers, Article 62(6) of the Battery Regulation requires the platform to obtain details of the relevant producer register and registration number or numbers, plus the producer’s self-certification committing to offer only batteries for which the specified EPR requirements are met.

The number remains the authority-issued number for the applicable Member State. Another identifier may be an internal lookup key but does not satisfy Article 62(6). The interaction with Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 must also be assessed; producer self-certification is not independent verification of compliance.

15.9 Reporting quantities placed on the market ​

Article 75 requires annual reporting to the competent authority by producers or, where appointed, PROs. Reporting is by category and chemistry and includes batteries incorporated in vehicles and appliances (Article 75(1)–(4)).

Reporting groupQuantities and outcomes
Portable and LMT batteriesAmount first made available; portable batteries of general use as a separate amount; amounts collected, delivered for treatment, exported for treatment or specified circular operations, and delivered for preparation for re-use or repurposing; and collection rate (Article 75(1)). Portable and LMT collection rates use the three-year-average method described in Annex XI (Articles 59(3) and 60(3); Annex XI)
SLI, industrial and EV batteriesAmount first made available and collected amounts delivered for preparation for re-use or repurposing, delivered for treatment, or exported for those operations or treatment (Article 75(2)).

Reporting is territorial. Batteries that leave the Member State in the same year before sale to end-users are excluded from the amount first made available. Portable and LMT collection rates use the three-year-average method.

Reports are due within six months after the reporting year. The first reporting period is the first full calendar year after entry into force of the implementing act establishing the Commission reporting format. Authorities establish electronic systems and specify formats and may request information needed to assure reliability (Article 75(7)–(9)). Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2289, which establishes that format, was published on 21 November 2025 and entered into force on the twentieth day after publication, 11 December 2025 (Article 2). The first reporting year is therefore 2026, with reports due by 30 June 2027. These dates are calculated from Article 75(7) and Article 2 of the implementing act; Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/606 likewise refers to Article 75(5) information commencing for calendar year 2026 (Annex, section 5, point 1). Article 1 of the implementing act sets the format Member States use to report to the Commission, and does not directly address operators. Operators report in the format set by the competent authorities of each Member State where they have reporting duties (Article 75(8)), so they need to check the current requirements of those authorities or of their producer responsibility organisation. The Commission format is still useful for planning, because it shows the data Member States have to supply (Article 76(1); Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2289, Annexes I to III).

15.10 How GLN complements official producer identification ​

The Regulation requires official identity information and an authority-issued registration number. A GLN complements those identifiers by providing a globally unique key for a legal entity, organisational function or location in business exchanges. In the GS1 System GLNs are distinguished for locations, parties legal entities.

15.11 How GTIN and structured data support reporting ​

The Regulation requires category-, chemistry- and weight-based reporting. GTIN, GDSN messages and other GS1 standards support this. In source transactions and master data, GTIN identifies the battery trade item or model, enabling quantities to be aggregated consistently.

The standard may be used as follows:

  1. Assign a GTIN to the supplied trade item. If a battery is the trade item, a GTIN should be used to identify it. If incorporated in or supplied with another product, the host-product GTIN identifies the host product with the battery. But the battery itself is also identified with a separate GTIN for the battery model, category, chemistry and weight and will remain separate linked data.
  2. Associate the GTIN with controlled data for category, chemistry, unit weight, incorporation status and responsible producer by Member State. A GTIN does not encode neither master data nor legal attributes.
  3. Capture transactions and adjustments by Member State and date, distinguishing first supply, movements out before sale to end-users, returns and circular operations.
  4. Aggregate records into authority-prescribed category, chemistry and weight measures while retaining reconciliation to source transactions.

Batch or lot and/or serial identifiers support reconciliation where already used. GTIN alone is not an individual battery identifier; it identifies the battery model. Instance identification normally requires GTIN plus serial number. The system must avoid double counting where battery and host product have separate GTINs or a battery passes through several actors.

GDSN supports standardised trade item master data exchange only where applicable attributes, validations and target-market implementations have been verified against current official documentation. GS1 standards improve interoperability and reconciliation but do not determine producer status, calculate liability by themselves, replace authority formats or establish compliance.

15.12 National implementation limitations ​

Before operation, the producer or representative needs to confirm for each Member State:

  • the competent authority, register and electronic application route;
  • whether registration and authorisation are combined;
  • whether appointment of a PRO is mandatory for the category;
  • permitted additional documents, procedural modalities, fees and language requirements;
  • how distributors and online platforms verify current registration;
  • collection-system, guarantee, contribution and enforcement arrangements;
  • reporting taxonomy, electronic format, validation rules and correction process; and
  • national measures for matters not fully harmonised by Chapter VIII.

Mappings between GLNs or GTINs and national EPR records require agreed governance among producers, PROs, distributors, platforms and reporting providers. Access controls must preserve commercial and industrial confidentiality.
