13. Labelling, QR codes and battery passports ​

This section explains the common rules for physical labels, markings, QR codes and battery passports. These are distinct mechanisms. A QR code is a physical data carrier, not a battery passport, and does not replace physical markings or CE marking.

13.1 Physical labels and markings ​

Article 13 creates duties with different scopes and dates. Manufacturers ensure Article 13 labelling for batteries they place on the market or put into service (Article 38(1)); importers and distributors have verification duties (Articles 41(2) and 42(2)).

ItemScope and ruleTiming or qualification
General labelAll batteries; Annex VI, Part A (Article 13(1))From 18 August 2026 or 18 months after entry into force of the Article 13(10) act, whichever is later; OQ-REG-001 remains open.
Capacity labelRechargeable portable, LMT and SLI batteries (Article 13(2))Same conditional start rule.
Duration and “non-rechargeable” labelsNon-rechargeable portable batteries (Article 13(3))Same conditional start rule.
Separate-collection symbolAll batteries (Article 13(4); Annex VI, Part B)From 18 August 2025; size and small-symbol packaging rules apply.
Cd or Pb symbolBatteries exceeding 0.002% cadmium or 0.004% lead (Article 13(5))Printed beneath the collection symbol; see OQ-REG-002 on transition.

The general label includes manufacturer identity; battery category and Article 38(6) identification; place and date of manufacture; weight; capacity; chemistry; specified hazardous substances; usable extinguishing agent; and critical raw materials above 0.1% by weight (Annex VI, Part A). Article 38(6) requires model identification with a batch or serial number, product number or another identifying element; it does not universally require serialisation.

Article 13(7) requires the Article 13(1)–(6) labels and QR code to be visible, legible and indelible. Specified circular operations require new labels or markings and QR access to status-change information (Article 13(9); Annex XIII, point 4); the non-passport implementation is unresolved under OQ-REG-007.

13.2 Physical CE marking ​

CE marking and QR access are separate. Article 20(1) requires CE marking visibly, legibly and indelibly on the battery or, where impossible or not warranted, on packaging and accompanying documents. It is affixed before placing on the market or putting into service (Article 20(2)); a notified-body number follows where Annex VIII requires it (Article 20(3)).

For batteries outside passport scope, the QR code gives access to the declaration of conformity under Article 13(6)(b). For passport batteries, the declaration is public passport information under Annex XIII, point 1(r), reached through Article 13(6)(a). QR access does not replace physical CE marking under Articles 19–20, and CE marking does not satisfy the QR duty.

13.3 The regulatory QR code requirement ​

From 18 February 2027 all batteries must bear a QR code (Article 13(6)). Annex VI, Part C requires high contrast and a size readable by a commonly available reader; Article 13(7) governs permanence and placement.

Battery CategoryReferenceThe role of QR code
LMT; industrial above 2 kWh; EVArticle 13(6)(a)Leads to battery passport
Other battery, including industrial at exactly 2 kWh or lessArticle 13(6)(b)Leads to applicable labels, declaration, report and other information.
SLIArticle 13(6)(b) and (c)Leads to “other battery” information plus applicable recycled-content information.

13.4 QR codes for batteries not requiring a battery passport ​

For batteries not requiring a battery passport the regulation requires access to applicable information, declaration, reports and waste information and that the information must be complete, up-to-date and accurate.

The Regulation does not prescribe the identification granularity behind the QR code (Article 13(6)). Where the required information is the same for the whole battery model, the QR code encodes a GS1 Digital Link URI with GTIN alone. Where that information varies by production batch, the URI should include GTIN plus batch/lot number. This is a guideline recommendation, not a legal requirement. The resolver describes this information with link type gs1:pip (Section 13.8.1). Further details are provided in Section 19.7, “Non-passport QR-code implementation”.

13.5 QR codes for batteries requiring a battery passport ​

For batteries that require a battery passport, the battery passport shall be accessible through the QR code, which links to a unique identifier that the economic operator placing the battery on the market shall attribute to it (Article 77(3)).

The QR code and the unique identifier shall comply with ISO/IEC 15459-1 to 15459-6, or their equivalent (Article 77(3)). The Regulation does not prescribe GS1 standards for this function. This guideline proposes a QR code encoding a GS1 Digital Link URI with GTIN plus serial number, which leads to the battery passport of that individual battery. The resolver describes the passport with link type gs1:dpp (Section 13.8.1). GS1 is an ISO/IEC 15459 conformant Issuing Agency (GS1 General Specifications, section 1.2.1). The operator remains responsible for checking the complete implementation against Article 77(3); see Section 19.8, “Battery-passport implementation”.

13.6 QR code placement options ​

The labels and the QR code shall be printed or engraved visibly, legibly and indelibly on the battery. Where this is not possible or not warranted on account of the nature and size of the battery, the labels and the QR code shall be affixed to the packaging and to the documents accompanying the battery.

13.7 GS1 Digital Link URI ​

GS1 provides a way to encode a unique identifier in a QR code and makes the information web accessible. A GS1 Digital Link is a URI encoding a GTIN and qualifiers including batch/lot and/or serial number.

The Regulation requires the battery passport or the battery information being accessible via QR code. GS1 Digital Link supports this functionality in an interoperable and globally unique manner. The standard is used as follows:

  1. Determine the identified object and model, batch or instance granularity. Keep an incorporated or supplied battery distinct from its host product.
  2. Allocate GTIN to the battery model (trade item) and add the appropriate qualifier (batch/lot or serial number) where finer granularity is required.
  3. Construct the URI in accordance with Section 4, “GS1 Digital Link URI syntax” (pp. 19–36), of the GS1 Digital Link URI Syntax standard.
  4. Encode this URI syntax in a QR code according to GS1 rules, meeting Article 13(7) and Annex VI, Part C.
  5. Redirect public requests to correct resources.
  6. Keep the GS1 identifier permanently associated with the same battery or battery model. Even if the information, service provider, website or resolver changes, do not reassign the identifier to another object.

This provides globally unique identification and a stable entry point.

Different organisations can operate a resolver.

13.8.1 Link types for the two QR routes ​

A resolver describes each resource it links to with a link type from the GS1 Web Vocabulary. This guideline recommends one link type for each QR route:

Battery CategoryQR routeLink typeResource
LMT; industrial above 2 kWh; EVArticle 13(6)(a)gs1:dpp (Digital Product Passport)The battery passport of the individual battery
Portable, including portable batteries of general use; SLI; industrial at 2 kWh or lessArticle 13(6)(b); for SLI also Article 13(6)(c)gs1:pip (Product Information Page)The page that gives access to the applicable information, declaration, report and waste information

Both are stable link types in GS1 Web Vocabulary version 1.18. gs1:dpp is defined as “a link to a digital product passport”. gs1:pip is defined as “a link to information specifically about the identified item, typically operated by the brand owner or a retailer of the product and aimed at consumers”.

The Regulation does not prescribe link types; this is a recommendation of this guideline. A link type describes a resource. It does not grant access to restricted passport information (Section 13.9). Further resources can be given other link types.

13.9 Multiple audiences and information resources ​

Scanning a QR code can direct to several resources depending on the audiences and their access levels. For example, a product landing page can expose public resources; access-based resources can serve authorities, notified bodies and legitimate-interest users.

13.10 Battery passport content and access ​

From 18 February 2027, each LMT battery, each industrial battery with a capacity greater than 2 kWh and each EV battery placed on the market or put into service has a battery passport (Article 77(1)). The passport holds information about the battery model and about the individual battery, including information resulting from its use. Each item applies to the extent that it is applicable to the battery category or sub-category concerned (Article 77(2)).

Annex XIII sets four sets of information, each with its own access rights:

Annex XIII pointLevelWho has accessInformation
Point 1Battery modelGeneral publicAnnex VI, Part A information; material composition, including chemistry, hazardous substances other than mercury, cadmium or lead, and critical raw materials; carbon-footprint information under Article 7(1) and (2); responsible-sourcing information from the Article 52(3) report; recycled-content information under Article 8(1); share of renewable content; rated capacity in Ah; minimal, nominal and maximum voltage; original power capability and limits; expected lifetime in cycles and the reference test; capacity threshold for exhaustion (EV batteries only); temperature range when not in use; period of the commercial warranty for calendar life; initial round-trip energy efficiency and at 50% of cycle life; internal cell and pack resistance; c-rate of the relevant cycle-life test; Article 13(4) and (5) markings; EU declaration of conformity; and Article 74(1)(a)–(f) information
Point 2Battery modelPersons with a legitimate interest, the Commission, notified bodies and market surveillance authoritiesDetailed composition, including materials in the cathode, anode and electrolyte; part numbers for components and contact details of sources for replacement spares; dismantling information; and safety measures
Point 3Battery modelNotified bodies, market surveillance authorities and the CommissionResults of test reports proving compliance
Point 4Individual batteryPersons with a legitimate interestArticle 10(1) performance and durability values when placed on the market and when its status changes; state of health under Article 14; status (“original”, “repurposed”, “re-used”, “remanufactured” or “waste”); and data from its use, including charging and discharging cycles, negative events such as accidents, and periodically recorded operating conditions and state of charge

Source: Article 77(2), second subparagraph; Annex XIII, points 1–4.

Legitimate-interest access serves two purposes. The first is dismantling and detailed model composition where essential for repairers, remanufacturers, second-life operators and recyclers to conduct their activities. The second, for individual batteries, is where essential for the purchaser, or parties acting on the purchaser's behalf, to make the battery available to independent energy aggregators or energy market participants (Article 77(2), third subparagraph). Article 77(9) requires implementing acts specifying these persons and their access, due by 18 August 2026. No adopted act was found when this section was written (OQ-REG-017).

Not every item exists for every battery from 18 February 2027. Items in point 1 follow their underlying provisions: carbon-footprint information follows the Article 7 dates, recycled-content information the Article 8 dates, and the due-diligence report the Article 47 scope and Article 48(1) date (Section 29.4, “Application dates”). State-of-health information in point 4 applies only where Article 14 applies: EV and LMT batteries and stationary battery energy storage systems, not every industrial battery above 2 kWh. The Commission's guidance on passport data points (version 3.2, October 2026) is an aid and does not replace these provisions.

13.11 Responsibility for the passport ​

The economic operator placing the battery on the market attributes the unique identifier and ensures that the passport information is accurate, complete and up to date. It may give another operator written authorisation to act on its behalf (Article 77(3)–(4)). Data is stored by the responsible operator or by operators it authorises, and those operators may not sell, re-use or process the data beyond what is necessary for the storing or processing service (Article 78(c)–(d)).

Responsibility changes at two points in the battery's life:

  • After a circular operation: for a battery subject to preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing, responsibility moves to the economic operator placing that battery on the market or putting it into service. The battery has a new passport linked to the passport or passports of the original battery or batteries (Article 77(7), first subparagraph).
  • When it becomes waste: responsibility moves to the producer, the appointed producer responsibility organisation or the waste management operator selected under Article 57(8) (Article 77(7), second subparagraph).

The passport ceases to exist after the battery has been recycled (Article 77(8)). Further details are provided in Section 17.5, “Battery-passport consequences”.

13.12 Data format, access rights and continuity ​

Passport information is based on open standards and is in an interoperable format, transferable through an open interoperable data exchange network without vendor lock-in, machine-readable, structured and searchable (Article 77(5)). Article 78 adds the following essential requirements:

13.13 Registering the unique identifier ​

The economic operator placing the battery on the market or putting it into service uploads the unique identifier to the registry established under Article 13(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 (Article 77(10)). This is separate from hosting the passport.

Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 (official text) sets out the arrangements for the registry and expressly covers batteries under Article 77 (Article 1(1)(b)). It entered into force on 6 August 2026. The relevant actor registers the passport at the level of granularity specified in the relevant Union law, through the registry's secure user interface or its API, and the registry generates a unique and persistent registration identifier (Article 8(2), (6) and (8)). The full operational rules need checking before deployment (OQ-REG-017).
