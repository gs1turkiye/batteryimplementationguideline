18. Data model for batteries ​

This section gives an overview of the information that exists about a battery under Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, attribute by attribute, for all six battery types. It shows what each attribute means, its data type, whether it applies to each battery type, and where this guideline explains the requirement behind it. The full list is in Annex A, the “Attributes” sheet of the workbook EU_battery_attribute_list_v0.5_GS1.xlsx.

For batteries with a passport, the attributes follow the European Commission's official battery data model. The Regulation requires passport information to be structured, machine-readable and interoperable (Article 77(5)), but it does not prescribe GS1 standards for this. This section does not map the attributes to GS1 standards.

18.1 How this overview was created ​

The overview is taken from the “Attributes” sheet of Annex A. That list was built in four steps:

  1. Commission data model. The 96 attributes in the Commission's battery data model, version 1.0.0 of 30 October 2026, are the starting point. The names, definitions and data types of these attributes come from that model.
  2. Regulation. The list was checked against the consolidated text of the Regulation of 13 August 2026 and its secondary acts. Commission guidance on passport data points, version 3.2 of 19 October 2026, was used to help interpret the data points. It states that it is not the Commission's official position.
  3. Additions. Attributes the Regulation requires but the Commission's model does not yet contain were added, including those for batteries without a passport. No Commission data model exists for those batteries, so the list reuses the Commission's terms where they fit.
  4. Check by battery type. Every attribute was checked for all six battery types against the Regulation.

Read the tables with these limits in mind:

  • The Regulation prevails. This overview is a reading aid. It does not create obligations or replace the legal text, and the Commission's data model is not a legal act.
  • A code is a summary. Conditions, dates and open questions are in the notes in Annex A. For example, carbon footprint information applies only to rechargeable industrial batteries (Article 7).
  • Not every attribute is QR-code information. Some are held in the battery management system, kept for authorities or reported by the producer.
  • The list is a draft. Attributes marked with an asterisk have not been reviewed by the Commission or GS1.

The “Attributes” sheet also shows each attribute's identifier, access rights, allowed units, code list, application date, legal basis, guidance data point and notes. Identifiers starting with GS1- mark the attributes added in step 3. The prefix marks the additions only: these attributes are not GS1 standard terms. The sheet “GS1 findings” records the check in step 4.

18.5 Limitations and open questions ​

The Commission's model has no attributes yet for carbon footprint, responsible sourcing and recycled content. Commission guidance version 3.2 says this information does not have to be filled in as of February 2027. The attribute names used here for them are proposals, and the Commission's names may differ.

The following questions affect this data model and are explained in Section 29.6, “Open legal and technical questions”: OQ-REG-001 (label specifications and date), OQ-REG-003 (QR handling of absent reports), OQ-REG-005 (capacity and stationary systems), OQ-REG-006 (SLI recovered-material information), OQ-REG-007 (status information outside the passport), OQ-REG-017 (passport access and predecessor links) and OQ-REG-022 (Annex XIII, point 1(t)).

The Commission's code list for battery chemistry has no codes for some common portable chemistries, such as alkaline and zinc-air. Annex A proposes additional codes, which are not Commission or GS1 codes.
