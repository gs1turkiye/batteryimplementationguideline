7. Starting, lighting and ignition batteries ​

7.1 Battery passport necessity ​

An SLI battery does not require a battery passport under Article 77(1), irrespective of capacity. From 18 February 2027, the Article 13(6)(b) QR-code route applies and must additionally provide access to the SLI-specific recycled-content information in Article 13(6)(c).

Further details on determining battery-passport applicability and implementing the relevant information requirements are provided in Section 3.4, “Determine whether a battery passport is required”, and Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”.

Common requirements—including restricted substances, technical documentation, conformity assessment, CE marking, economic-operator duties and market surveillance—are addressed in Sections 10 and 11.

Article 11 does not impose its removability and replaceability duties on SLI batteries; it addresses portable and LMT batteries. Where an SLI battery is incorporated in a vehicle or other product, the battery and host product remain distinct regulated objects. End-of-life vehicle and WEEE treatment facilities must hand resulting waste batteries to the responsible producer, producer responsibility organisation or selected waste management operator and keep records (Article 65).

7.3 Recycled content ​

StageApplication triggerProvision
Recovered-content documentation18 August 2028 or 24 months after the Article 8(1) delegated act enters into force, whichever is laterArticle 8(1)
First minimum shares18 August 2031Article 8(2)
Increased minimum shares18 August 2036Article 8(3)

For SLI batteries containing the relevant materials within Article 8’s scope, records operate at battery-model, calendar-year and manufacturing-plant level. Article 8(2) sets minimum shares of 16% cobalt, 85% lead, 6% lithium and 6% nickel; Article 8(3) raises these to 26% cobalt, 85% lead, 12% lithium and 15% nickel. The values remain subject to lawful amendment.

Article 8(4) excludes batteries already marketed or put into service before preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing. Check the delegated methodology and document format before treating the conditional disclosure date as operative. Further details on product, sustainability and safety requirements are provided in Section 10, “Product, sustainability and safety requirements”.

7.4 Labelling, markings and QR-code information ​

In addition to common Article 13 requirements:

  • a rechargeable SLI battery must bear capacity information from 18 August 2026 or 18 months after the Article 13(10) implementing act enters into force, whichever is later (Article 13(2));
  • from 18 February 2027, its QR code must provide access to applicable Article 13(1)–(5) information, the EU declaration of conformity, any applicable Article 52(3) due-diligence report, and Article 74(1)(a)–(f) waste information (Article 13(6)(b)); and
  • the QR code must also provide access to the amount of cobalt, lead, lithium or nickel recovered from waste and present in active materials, calculated under Article 8 (Article 13(6)(c)).

Where the battery’s nature and size prevent printing or engraving on it, Article 13(7) requires the label or QR code on the packaging and accompanying documents. Because the Article 13(6)(c) QR duty begins before the base-text Article 8(1) disclosure date, the information required during that interval remains unresolved (OQ-REG-006).

7.5 Due diligence, producer responsibility and end of life ​

Battery due diligence is not determined by passport necessity. It applies subject to the threshold and exclusions in Article 47 and Articles 48–52. Its application date and further details are provided in Section 14.1, “Scope and applicable economic operators”.

A producer must register in each Member State in which it first makes an SLI battery available and may not supply there unless registered (Article 55(1), (2) and (12)). Producers or appointed producer responsibility organisations must take back, free of charge and without requiring a new purchase, all waste SLI batteries of the category they first made available in that Member State, irrespective of nature, chemistry, condition, brand or origin. They must provide territory-wide arrangements, suitable infrastructure and delivery to permitted treatment facilities (Article 61(1)–(4)). Article 61 sets no percentage collection target for SLI batteries.

Distributors must take back waste SLI batteries of categories they offer or offered, subject to Article 62, including its distance-sale and delivery rules. Annual producer and waste-operator reporting applies by category and chemistry (Article 75(2)–(3)). Further details on producer responsibility, waste collection and reporting are provided in Section 15, “Producer registration and extended producer responsibility”, and Section 16, “Waste collection, treatment and recycling”.

After a listed circular operation, the operator placing the battery on the market or putting it into service is treated as the manufacturer (Articles 38(11) and 45). This does not create an Article 77 passport. Article 13(9) may, however, require new labelling or marking and QR-accessible status-change information. Further details on obligations following circular operations are provided in Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”.

7.6 GS1 implementation for SLI batteries ​

The Regulation requires identification and information-access functions but does not prescribe GS1 standards.

FunctionHow GS1 standards support itGranularity and limitation
Identify the battery modelGTIN identifies the battery model or trade itemIt must identify the battery, not merely the host vehicle or machine. A GTIN alone is not an individual identifier.
Associate Article 8 recordsA GTIN, production year and manufacturing-plant GLN associate model/year/plant recordsA GLN does not replace legally required plant information or an authority-issued identifier.
Distinguish a production batchGTIN plus batch or lot number identifies a batchThis can support traceability but does not create a passport or satisfy technical-documentation duties by itself.
Provide web accessA GS1 Digital Link URI connects the identified battery to controlled resources, described with link type gs1:pip (Section 13.8.1)SLI batteries have no battery passport, so the URI connects to the Article 13(6)(b)–(c) information and not to a passport. That information must remain complete, current and accurate.
Identify parties and locationsGLNs identify manufacturers, distributors, collection points and treatment sitesA GLN does not replace a producer-registration number or other legally issued identifier.
Identify logistic unitsSSCC identifies a logistic unit containing SLI batteriesIt does not identify the contained batteries; their relationship must be recorded.
Share visibility eventsEPCIS captures events concerning batteries, logistic units, parties and locationsEPCIS does not prove compliance or replace conformity assessment, verification, certification, audit or an authority decision.

Because SLI batteries do not require individual passports, granularity follows the supported function. Model/year/plant granularity is material for Article 8; batch identification supports production and recall controls; serialisation serves individual traceability where it is operationally needed. Where an SLI battery is incorporated in or supplied with a vehicle or machine, identify the battery and host product separately and maintain their relationship.

Further details on GS1 implementation methods for identification, information access and traceability are provided in Section 12, “Battery and grouping identification”; Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”; and Section 20, “Supply-chain traceability and information exchange”.

7.7 Category-specific example ​

For a rechargeable lead-acid SLI battery supplied for a vehicle, GTIN identifies the battery trade item, GTIN plus batch or lot number distinguishes its production batch, and GLN identifies the manufacturing plant for association with Article 8 model/year/plant records. From 18 February 2027, a GS1 Digital Link URI in the QR code provides access to the applicable Article 13(6)(b) and (c) resources.

The host vehicle and battery remain separately identified. The physical capacity, separate-collection, Pb and CE markings remain subject to their own provisions.

7.9 Limitations and open questions ​

SLI classification depends on specific design purpose, not weight, capacity or chemistry alone. A battery used for auxiliary or backup power remains an SLI battery only where it is specifically designed for the Article 3(1), point (12) starting, lighting or ignition function.

Current-law checks remain necessary for the label act (OQ-REG-001), Cd/Pb transition (OQ-REG-002), QR treatment of an inapplicable due-diligence report (OQ-REG-003), SLI QR information before Article 8 disclosure applies (OQ-REG-006), circular-operation QR information (OQ-REG-007), national reporting formats (Section 15.9) and collection-method amendments (OQ-REG-013). GS1 identifiers and EPCIS support interoperability but do not establish legal compliance.
