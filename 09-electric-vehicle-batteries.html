9. Electric vehicle batteries ​

9.1 Battery passport necessity ​

Every EV battery within the temporal scope of Article 77(1) requires a battery passport; no capacity threshold applies to this category.

Further details on the battery-passport definition, its application from 18 February 2027, its relationship with the QR code and unique identifier, and its treatment following circular operations are provided in Section 28.1, “Regulatory terms”. Further details on implementation are provided in Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”.

9.2 Product and sustainability requirements ​

The following requirements have a specific or distinct application to EV batteries:

RequirementEffect on EV batteriesProvision
Carbon footprintDeclaration for each battery model per manufacturing plant; at later conditional dates, a carbon-footprint performance-class label and a maximum life-cycle carbon-footprint thresholdArticle 7(1)–(3); Annex II
Recycled contentDocumentation of the percentages of cobalt, lithium and nickel present in active materials and recovered from battery manufacturing waste or post-consumer waste, and of lead present in the battery and recovered from waste; at later dates, minimum recovered-content sharesArticle 8(1)–(3)
Performance and durabilityDocument accompanying the battery with the applicable electrochemical performance and durability parameter values; technical documentation explaining how those values were determinedArticle 10(1); Annex IV, Part A
State of health and expected lifetimeCurrent state-of-health and expected-lifetime data in the battery management system; read-only access for specified purposes; software reset functionality for circular operationsArticle 14; Annex VII

Article 10(1) has applied since 18 August 2024. It requires the EV battery to be accompanied by the stated performance and durability values and the technical documentation to explain how they were measured, calculated or estimated. Article 10 does not establish later minimum performance values for EV batteries.

Article 14 has applied since 18 August 2024. The person who legally purchased the battery, including independent operators and waste management operators, or a person acting on that purchaser’s behalf, is to receive read-only, non-discriminatory access to the battery-management-system data for the purposes specified in Article 14(2). The battery management system and the battery passport are related information sources but are not the same system.

Requirements common to several battery categories—including restricted substances, conformity assessment, CE marking, manufacturer, importer and distributor duties, and market surveillance—are addressed in Sections 10 and 11.

9.3 Carbon footprint and recycled content ​

Article 7 establishes three conditional stages for EV batteries:

StageApplication triggerProvision
Carbon-footprint declaration18 February 2025 or 12 months after the relevant methodology and format acts enter into force, whichever is laterArticle 7(1)
Carbon-footprint value and performance-class label18 August 2026 or 18 months after the relevant class and format acts enter into force, whichever is laterArticle 7(2)
Maximum life-cycle carbon-footprint threshold18 February 2028 or 18 months after the relevant delegated act enters into force, whichever is laterArticle 7(3)

The declaration is prepared for each battery model per manufacturing plant. Until it is accessible through the QR code, it accompanies the battery.

For EV batteries containing the relevant materials, Article 8 requires recovered-material documentation from 18 August 2028 or 24 months after the Article 8(1) delegated act enters into force, whichever is later (Article 8(1)–(3)). Minimum recovered-content shares apply from 18 August 2031 and increase from 18 August 2036.

The Article 7 and 8 exemptions for batteries already placed on the market or put into service before a listed circular operation are preserved in Articles 7(5) and 8(4). Applicable delegated and implementing acts must be checked before a conditional date, methodology, format or value is treated as operative.

Further details on product, sustainability and safety requirements are provided in Section 10, “Product, sustainability and safety requirements”.

9.4 Labelling, QR code and passport information ​

In addition to common Article 13 markings, EV batteries have the following category-specific information requirements:

  • the carbon-footprint label applies on the conditional date in Article 7(2);
  • the QR code provides access to the battery passport from 18 February 2027 (Article 13(6)(a)); and
  • the public passport information includes an EV-specific capacity threshold for exhaustion (Annex XIII, point 1(k)).

The EV-specific result is that passport information includes this capacity threshold for exhaustion. The general passport content, access layers, governance duties and distinctions between the QR code, passport, unique identifier, physical markings and CE marking are defined in Section 28.1, “Regulatory terms”, and explained in Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”. Further details on the responsible actors are provided in Section 11, “Economic operators, conformity and market surveillance”.

9.5 Due diligence, producer responsibility and end of life ​

Battery due diligence is not triggered by passport scope alone. It applies subject to the economic-operator threshold and exclusions in Article 47 and the requirements in Articles 48–52. Its application date and further details are provided in Section 14.1, “Scope and applicable economic operators”.

A producer must register in each Member State in which it first makes an EV battery available and may not supply there unless registered (Article 55(1), (2) and (12)). Producers or appointed producer responsibility organisations must accept all waste EV batteries of the category they made available, free of charge and without requiring a new purchase (Article 61(1)). Article 61 does not establish a percentage collection target for EV batteries. Annual category-and-chemistry reporting applies under Article 75(2). Further details on producer responsibility, waste collection and reporting are provided in Sections 15 and 16.

After a listed circular operation, the operator placing the battery on the market or putting it into service is treated as the manufacturer (Articles 38(11) and 45). A new passport linked to the original passport or passports is required under Article 77(7). A battery repurposed for a different use must be classified according to its new intended use; this may change the requirements and passport threshold. Further details on obligations following circular operations are provided in Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”.

9.6 GS1 implementation for EV batteries ​

FunctionHow GS1 standards support itGranularity and limitation
Identify the battery model and individual batteryGTIN identifies the battery when managed as a trade item; GTIN plus serial number identifies the individual battery instanceGTIN alone does not identify an individual instance. The complete identifier and QR implementation must satisfy Article 77(3).
Provide web-enabled accessA GS1 Digital Link URI expresses the GTIN and serial number and connects the identified battery to controlled information resources; the passport is described with link type gs1:dpp (Section 13.8.1)The URI and resolver are not the battery passport. Article 77–78 access, persistence, security and governance requirements remain applicable.
Identify organisations and sitesGLNs identify parties, functions and locations such as manufacturing or treatment sitesA GLN does not replace an authority-issued registration or other legal identifier.
Identify logistic unitsSSCC identifies a logistic unit containing one or more batteriesSSCC does not identify the contained battery instances; their relationship to the logistic unit must be recorded.
Share lifecycle visibility dataEPCIS captures and shares events concerning identified batteries, logistic units, parties and locationsEPCIS does not prove compliance or replace the passport, conformity assessment, verification, certification, audit or an authority decision.

Because each EV battery requires an individual passport, the implementation must support instance-level association. A GS1 implementation will normally use GTIN plus serial number for the battery instance, while retaining model and, where relevant, batch or lot relationships. The battery and host vehicle remain separately identified objects and their relationship should be maintained explicitly.

Further details on GS1 implementation methods for identification, information access and traceability are provided in Section 12, “Battery and grouping identification”; Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”; and Section 20, “Supply-chain traceability and information exchange”.

9.8 Limitations and open questions ​

The definitions and distinctions in Section 28, “Glossary”, apply. GS1 identifiers and EPCIS support interoperability but do not establish legal compliance.

Current-law checks remain necessary for national reporting formats (Section 15.9) and end-of-waste technical requirements (OQ-REG-012) and passport access measures.
