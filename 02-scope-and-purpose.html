2. Scope, purpose and document boundaries ​

This guideline explains how GS1 standards support the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 across all battery categories. It connects regulatory requirements with practical approaches to identifying batteries and supply-chain actors, linking information to the correct battery and sharing that information between organisations.

It is intended for manufacturers, importers, distributors, producers and other professional operators responsible for batteries, together with the teams and service providers implementing their identification and information systems. It helps readers understand which regulatory functions their systems need to support, what objects and data are involved, and how a GS1 implementation can be organised. Section 3, “How to use this guideline”, explains how to find the guidance relevant to a battery category and an organisation's role.

2.1 Regulatory scope ​

The guideline covers the Battery Regulation's requirements for sustainability, safety, labelling, marking and information, together with battery due diligence, extended producer responsibility and waste-battery collection, treatment and reporting (Article 1(1)–(2)). Its focus is the identification, information access and data-sharing functions associated with those requirements.

The detailed sections explain the regulatory outcome first, followed by the relevant GS1 application and its implementation. They distinguish the required function from the choice of a GS1 standard, and explain the identification level, information relationships and qualifications needed for that use. Product testing, calculation methods and assessment procedures are described to establish their information needs; this guideline does not provide the procedures for performing them.

2.2 Product and battery scope ​

The guideline covers portable, light means of transport (LMT), starting, lighting and ignition (SLI), industrial and electric vehicle (EV) batteries. The Regulation's general scope applies regardless of battery shape, volume, weight, design, material composition, chemistry, use or purpose, subject to its express exclusions (Article 1(3), (5)–(6)).

Coverage includes portable batteries of general use, button cells and stationary battery energy storage systems, as well as rechargeable and non-rechargeable configurations. Portable batteries of general use are a subcategory of portable batteries, and stationary battery energy storage systems are industrial batteries (Article 3(1), points (9)–(15)). Section 4, “Determining the battery category”, contains the definitions and classification boundaries.

Cells and modules supplied for end use without further incorporation or assembly into larger batteries are treated as batteries, with the requirements for the most similar category applying; where more than one category is possible, Article 1(4) specifies the stricter outcome.

The guideline also addresses batteries following preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing. These operations are included in the battery definition and can change the applicable responsibilities and information requirements (Article 3(1), point (1); Articles 38(11) and 45). Further details on responsibilities and information requirements following circular operations are provided in Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”.

2.3 Batteries incorporated in or supplied with products ​

Coverage includes batteries incorporated in, supplied with or specifically designed to be incorporated in or added to other products (Article 1(3)). The relevant battery requirements therefore need to be assessed even when the commercial product supplied is an appliance, vehicle or other item of equipment.

The guideline treats the battery and the host product as distinct identification and data objects. An implementation needs to make clear which information describes the battery, which describes the host product and how their relationship is recorded. The appropriate relationship may concern a model, a production batch/lot or an individual instance. Section 12, “Battery and grouping identification”, explains these identification choices and their application to separately traded batteries, incorporated batteries and combined supply configurations.

2.4 Batteries subject to a mandatory battery passport ​

From 18 February 2027, each LMT battery, each industrial battery with a capacity greater than 2 kWh and each EV battery placed on the market or put into service requires a battery passport (Article 77(1)). The capacity threshold applies to industrial batteries; Article 77(1) sets no capacity threshold for LMT or EV batteries.

The guideline covers the identification and information arrangements supporting these passports, including access through the QR code, information associated with the individual battery and relationships following circular operations (Article 13(6)(a); Article 77(2)–(7)). Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”, provides the common legal and implementation detail.

2.5 Batteries outside the mandatory battery passport scope ​

Portable batteries, including portable batteries of general use, SLI batteries and industrial batteries with a capacity of 2 kWh or less do not require a passport under Article 77(1). They remain within this guideline and, subject to the exclusions in Section 2.6, the QR-code requirements applying from 18 February 2027.

For these batteries, the QR code provides access to the applicable labelling information, EU declaration of conformity, due-diligence report and waste-prevention and management information specified in Article 13(6)(b). SLI batteries also have the recovered-material information requirement in Article 13(6)(c). The detailed guidance addresses the conditions affecting the underlying information duties, including cases where a due-diligence report is not required.

This coverage supports information access and traceability for batteries outside passport scope. A QR code providing the required information does not itself constitute a battery passport. Further details on QR-accessible information for batteries outside mandatory passport scope are provided in Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”.

2.6 Exclusions from the Regulation ​

The Regulation excludes batteries incorporated in, or specifically designed for incorporation in, equipment connected with the protection of Member States' essential security interests, arms, munitions and war material. This exclusion does not extend to products that are not intended for specifically military purposes. Batteries incorporated in or specifically designed for equipment intended to be sent into space are also excluded (Article 1(5)).

Equipment specifically designed for the safety of nuclear installations has a narrower exclusion: Chapters III and IX do not apply to it. Chapter VIII on waste batteries still applies. This is not an exclusion from the whole Regulation (Article 1(6), as corrected by OJ L 90243, 17.4.2024).

These exclusions differ from a category-specific threshold or derogation affecting an individual requirement. Such qualifications are explained with the requirement concerned.

2.7 Relationship with other Union and national legislation ​

The Battery Regulation is the legal basis for the battery requirements explained here. The guideline addresses other Union or national rules where their interaction is necessary to understand a battery requirement, but does not provide a complete assessment of the legislation applying to a battery, host product, facility or organisation.

For example, the Regulation's substance restrictions operate alongside the legislation identified in Article 6(1), and operators placing batteries on the market after specified circular operations must consider the relevant product, environmental, health-protection and transport requirements identified in Article 45(2). Producer registration also requires attention to the Member States in which batteries are first made available (Article 55(1)–(2)). The relevant subject sections explain these interfaces where they affect the implementation described.

2.8 Limitations of this guideline ​

This guideline provides implementation guidance, not a complete system specification or an assessment of an individual operator's compliance. Readers need to verify application dates, secondary legislation and technical dependencies for their implementation. The open questions below remain unresolved.

Current-law checks remain necessary for Article 11 guidance and any adopted derogations (OQ-REG-004), national reporting formats (Section 15.9), collection-rate methodology or target amendments (OQ-REG-013) and passport access measures.
