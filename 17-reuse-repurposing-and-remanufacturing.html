17. Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing ​

17.1 Definitions and distinction between operations ​

Regulation (Article 3(1), (29)–(32)), distinguishes the operations by waste status, intended purpose and technical outcome. Re-use concerns a used battery and does not itself amount to a new placing on the market. Preparation for re-use concerns a waste battery prepared for use again without other pre-processing. Preparation for repurposing concerns a waste battery prepared for a different purpose or application. Repurposing concerns a battery that is not waste used for a different purpose or application. Remanufacturing concerns a used battery disassembled and evaluated at cell and module level, restored to at least 90 % of original rated capacity, with no more than 3 % difference in state of health between individual cells, for its original purpose or application.

Batteries intended for industrial use after preparation for repurposing or repurposing are industrial batteries (Article 3(1), point (13)). Classification must therefore be performed after the operation. Further details on determining the battery category are provided in Section 4, “Determining the battery category”.

17.2 When a battery is placed on the market again ​

A used battery undergoing only re-use is considered to have been placed on the market when first made available. A battery subjected to preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing is placed on the market anew (Article 38(11), read with recital 16).

An imported used battery is placed on the Union market when it first enters the Union. An imported battery that has undergone a listed circular operation must comply with applicable requirements when entering the Union (recital 16). Category, capacity and intended use after the operation determine the applicable requirements; the original conformity assessment, category and passport outcome cannot simply be assumed to remain sufficient.

17.3 Responsibility for regulatory compliance ​

An operator that carries out a listed circular operation and places the resulting battery on the market or puts it into service is considered a manufacturer (Article 38(11)). It must perform applicable manufacturer duties, including technical documentation, conformity assessment, the EU declaration of conformity, CE marking, identification, instructions, safety information and labelling (Articles 17–20 and 38).

The regulation mentions that examination, performance testing, packing and shipment of the battery and affected components must follow adequate quality-control and safety instructions. It further requires compliance with the Batteries Regulation and relevant Union product, environmental, human-health and transport-safety law, taking account of any category change. A remanufacturing operator must provide authorities, on request, documentation which meets Article 3(1), point (32).

Article 47 excludes batteries already placed on the market before the listed operation from Chapter VII due-diligence obligations. It does not remove duties under Articles 38 and 45 or other applicable requirements.

17.4 Labelling and QR-code consequences ​

Article 13(9) requires a battery subjected to a listed circular operation to bear new labels or markings. They must contain information on the change of status and make it accessible through the QR code. Annex XIII, point 4(c), lists “original”, “repurposed”, “re-used”, “remanufactured” and “waste”. In the same Article it is mentioned how physical placement of labels and QR code, including alternative placement on packaging and accompanying documents where marking the battery is impossible or unwarranted because of its nature and size.

For a battery within Article 77(1), the QR code provides access to the new passport. For all other batteries, Article 13(6)(b) applies from 18 February 2027 and Article 13(9) still requires QR access to status-change information.

17.5 Battery-passport consequences ​

From 18 February 2027, each LMT battery, each industrial battery above 2 kWh and each EV battery placed on the market or put into service requires a battery passport. After a listed circular operation, the responsibility for the accuracy, completeness and currency of passport information is transferred to the operator placing the resulting battery on the market or putting it into service. The battery must have a new passport linked to the passport or passports of the original battery or batteries.

Passport scope must be reassessed after the operation. A battery intended for industrial use after preparation for repurposing or repurposing is an industrial battery, which requires a passport only if its capacity is greater than 2 kWh. LMT and EV batteries within their definitions have no capacity threshold.

When a passport battery becomes waste, responsibility transfers to the producer, appointed producer responsibility organisation or selected waste-management operator, as applicable. The passport ceases to exist after recycling (Article 77(7)–(8)).

17.6 End-of-waste evidence ​

Article 73 applies to waste LMT, industrial and EV batteries subjected to preparation for re-use or preparation for repurposing. To demonstrate on request that a battery is no longer waste, its holder must provide:

  • a record of state-of-health evaluation or testing carried out in a Member State, confirming performance relevant to the further use;
  • an invoice or contract documenting sale or transfer of ownership for further use; and
  • evidence of protection against damage in transport, loading and unloading, including sufficient packaging and appropriate stacking.

The state-of-health record must accompany the battery and be available to end-users and persons acting on their behalf on equal terms and conditions; commercially sensitive information remains protected (Article 73(1)–(3)). Article 73 does not supply a general end-of-waste test for portable or SLI batteries.

17.7 Retaining and changing identifiers ​

The resulting manufacturer must provide model identification and a batch or serial number, product number or another identifying element (Article 38(6)). GTIN plus a batch/lot or serial number provides this identification.

Within the GS1 system, the operator assesses whether the result is a new trade item or a change to an existing trade item. Changes to declared formulation or functionality, net content, dimensions, gross weight or certification marking may trigger a new GTIN under the applicable rule.

GTIN plus serial number identifies an individual instance; GTIN alone does not. GTIN plus batch or lot number supports batch identification. Resulting batteries, removed cells, replacement components, packaging and logistic units are distinct objects and require identification at the level appropriate to each process.

17.8 Linking the original and subsequent battery identity ​

For an Article 77 battery, linking the new passport to the passport or passports of the original battery or batteries is mandatory; the Regulation does not prescribe GS1 as the linking method. The link must remain available even where a new GTIN and batch and/or serial number is allocated and may need to represent one-to-one, one-to-many or many-to-one lineage.

For batteries outside Article 77(1), maintaining this relationship is an operational traceability measure, not an Article 77 passport duty. It may support conformity evidence, status history, safety investigations and recall scope. Access controls should avoid exposing confidential data beyond the applicable legal or business purpose.

17.10 EPCIS supports lifecycle event records ​

The regulation states the need to retain evidence about examination, testing, movement, status change and lineage. An implementation based on EPCIS for events concerning identified batteries, components, logistic units, parties and locations helps a lot and captures and shares those events.

Events record receipt, inspection, state-of-health testing, disassembly, transformation inputs and outputs, assembly, identifier commissioning, packing, shipping and disposition change. EPCIS records what, when, where, why and, where relevant, how; TransformationEvent expresses input-output relationships and AssociationEvent expresses associations between hierarchies of trade items. GS1 CBV Standard (CBV) defines terms to describe inspecting, repairing, replacing, disassembling or commissioning, but does not reproduce the legal categories.

Event data should identify the original and resulting instances, relevant inputs and outputs, responsible party and facility, time, business process, disposition, document references and corrections. EPCIS access controls helps to restrict sensitive information. EPCIS records do not prove end-of-waste status, remanufacturing, conformity or passport compliance and do not replace required documents.
