22. Summary of regulatory functions and GS1 support ​

GS1 standards help operators connect batteries, responsible parties and regulatory information across all battery categories. The implementation starts with the applicable function: identifying a battery model, retrieving information through a QR code, maintaining an individual passport, tracing supplies or preparing reporting evidence. The same identifier connects these activities, but each activity retains its own data, responsibility and access requirements.

The tables below consolidate the category outcomes and common implementation principles. The Regulation does not prescribe GS1 standards, and they do not replace conformity assessment, verification, official registration or the underlying evidence. The technical sources in Section 22.4 support the GS1 applications throughout this section.

22.1 Summary by battery category ​

From 18 February 2027, Article 77(1) requires a passport for each LMT battery, industrial battery above 2 kWh and EV battery placed on the market or put into service. Article 13(6) requires QR-code access for all batteries within the Regulation's scope from that date. The capacity threshold applies to industrial batteries; Article 77(1) does not impose it on LMT or EV batteries.

Battery category or configurationPassport and QR outcomeGS1 implementation implicationDetailed guidance
Portable batteriesNo mandatory passport. QR access follows Article 13(6)(b).GTIN with the appropriate batch/lot qualification connects battery information to production records. Individual serialisation is not a general consequence of the QR duty.Section 5, “Portable batteries”.
Portable batteries of general useSame non-passport outcome as other portable batteries. Article 9 adds performance and durability provisions, subject to its exclusions and application conditions.The battery GTIN links to model-level performance documentation, and batch identification distinguishes affected batches where the evidence varies. This is a subcategory, not a separate passport class.Section 5.3, “Additional requirements for portable batteries of general use”.
LMT batteriesMandatory passport and QR access under Articles 77(1) and 13(6)(a).GTIN plus serial number and a GS1 Digital Link URI connect each battery to its passport, while sharing common model information.Section 6, “Light means of transport batteries”.
SLI batteriesNo mandatory passport. Article 13(6)(b) information is supplemented by the recycled-content information in Article 13(6)(c), calculated in accordance with Article 8.A GS1 Digital Link URI connects model or batch information. Recycled-content evidence needs an explicit relationship to the batteries it describes; the timing question is retained in Section 22.7.Section 7, “Starting, lighting and ignition batteries”.
Industrial batteries at or below 2 kWhNo mandatory passport; Article 13(6)(b) applies. Exactly 2 kWh is outside Article 77(1).Model and batch identification support information access and traceability. A serial identifier supports an operational need without creating a passport duty.Section 8, “Industrial batteries”.
Industrial batteries above 2 kWhMandatory passport and QR access under Articles 77(1) and 13(6)(a). The passport threshold does not contain a rechargeability condition.GTIN plus serial number identifies the individual battery and links model, batch and lifecycle information to that identity.Section 8, “Industrial batteries”.
Stationary battery energy storage systemsIndustrial-battery passport outcome depends on capacity. Articles 12 and 14 also impose specific safety and battery-management-system requirements.The appropriate battery identifiers connect technical documentation and individual system records. Web access does not replace required battery-management-system data access.Section 8.2, “Product, sustainability and safety requirements”.
EV batteriesMandatory passport and QR access under Articles 77(1) and 13(6)(a).GTIN plus serial number identifies the battery, and its relationship to the vehicle is recorded. The vehicle identity does not substitute for the battery identity.Section 9, “Electric vehicle batteries”.

For a battery whose category is uncertain, or an industrial battery whose capacity is unknown, passport status requires further information. Use Section 4, “Determining the battery category”, before choosing the implementation. Button-cell form does not itself determine passport status; classify the battery using Article 3(1).

Batteries incorporated in or supplied with products remain within scope under Article 1(3). Maintain the battery and host product as separate data objects, with an explicit relationship between them. After circular operations, reassess category and capacity and apply Articles 13(9), 38(11), 45 and 77(7) as relevant; the operation alone does not extend Article 77(1) to every battery. See Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”.

22.2 Summary by economic-operator role ​

A business may hold several roles. In particular, manufacturer status and producer status for extended producer responsibility are separate assessments (Article 3(1), points (33) and (47)). The following applications concern all battery categories unless a narrower scope is stated.

Actor and regulatory functionProvisionHow GS1 standards support the actor
Manufacturer: identify batteries, prepare conformity documentation and provide required markings and information.Article 38(1)–(7); Articles 17–20.Use GTIN with batch/lot or serial data as appropriate to connect the identified batteries to controlled documentation. Maintain the manufacturer's required name and contact details; GLN links the party record across systems.
Cell or module supplier: provide the manufacturer with information and documentation necessary for compliance.Article 39.Use identifiers for supplied trade items and the relevant batch or instance to associate evidence with the inputs concerned. Preserve the relationship to the finished battery where needed.
Authorised representative: perform tasks specified in the written mandate.Article 40(1)–(3).Use GLN and linked mandate records to distinguish the representative from the manufacturer and identify the batteries covered. The identifier does not establish the mandate or transfer excluded manufacturer duties.
Importer and distributor: perform their respective checks and respond to non-conformity.Articles 41(2), (6)–(8) and 42(2), (5)–(6).Match supplied battery identifiers to declarations, supplier records and corrective-action notices. Batch or instance records narrow a withdrawal or recall where supported by evidence.
Fulfilment service provider: ensure that its handling conditions do not jeopardise conformity.Article 43.Use SSCC for logistic units and recorded content relationships; EPCIS captures relevant handling observations. The operational record needs to identify the batteries affected.
Economic operator placing a passport-scope battery on the market: ensure passport information is accurate, complete and current.Article 77(3)–(6).Use an individual battery identifier and GS1 Digital Link URI as the access key, supported by a separately governed passport service. Written authorisation of another operator does not make the URI itself the passport.
Producer, or the appointed organisation or representative within the applicable arrangements: register and fulfil EPR duties.Articles 55–58.Link GLN party records to the authority-issued registration number and Member State. Associate battery GTINs with category, chemistry, battery mass and market records to support reporting preparation.
Treatment-facility operator covered by Article 65: record handovers of batteries removed from end-of-life vehicles or waste electrical and electronic equipment.Article 65(1)–(2).Use GLN for parties and sites, and SSCC for a managed logistic unit where suitable, linked to handover records. Record measured quantities and known battery identities without inventing missing identities.
Operator placing a battery on the market or putting it into service after specified circular operations: assume the relevant manufacturer and lifecycle duties.Articles 38(11), 45 and 77(7).Retain links between original and subsequent identities, reassess GTIN allocation and record the operation. For passport-scope batteries, maintain the required link between new and original passports.

See Section 11, “Economic operators, conformity and market surveillance”, and Section 15, “Producer registration and extended producer responsibility”, for the role-specific conditions.

22.4 Summary of GS1 standards and their use ​

The sources below are official GS1 standards held in the project source set. Their requirements govern the selected GS1 implementation. The Regulation does not prescribe these standards.

Standard or identifierUse in the applications aboveOfficial technical source
GTINIdentifies a trade item. Allocate it to the battery trade item and maintain its relationship to the regulatory battery model and documentation.GS1 General Specifications, release 26.0, section 1.3.1, pp. 35–36.

GTIN Management StandardGoverns whether a new or changed trade item needs a new GTIN. Apply its complete rules when products, packaging configurations or functionality change.GS1 GTIN Management Standard, release 1.1, sections 1–2, pp. 8–18; see Section 12.7, “GTIN management considerations”.

CBV provides standard vocabulary used with EPCIS; see Section 20.9, “EPCIS event data”, and the GS1 Core Business Vocabulary Standard cited there. GDSN supports standardised trade item master data exchange, but no complete battery-regulation attribute mapping is established here. Its use depends on verification of the official data model, target-market profiles and battery-versus-host attribute ownership; see Section 20.11, “GDSN and master-data exchange”.

22.5 Identification-level summary ​

Choose the level at which the information remains true and the responsible actor can retrieve the required evidence. The following levels describe implementation choices, not a requirement to apply every level to every battery.

LevelIdentified object and GS1 structureAppropriate use and boundary
Model or trade itemBattery trade item: GTIN.Shared product information and model documentation. Preserve the mapping to the regulatory model; GTIN alone does not identify an individual battery.
BatchProduction batch/lot: GTIN plus batch/lot number.Batch-specific production information, evidence and affected-stock searches. It does not distinguish batteries within the batch.
InstanceIndividual battery: GTIN plus serial number.Individual passport and lifecycle records. Common model information can remain shared while individual data is maintained separately.
Packaging trade itemA traded pack or case: its own GTIN under the applicable allocation rules.Ordering and master data for that packaging configuration. Retain the battery identity and quantity relationship; a pack GTIN is not automatically the contained battery's identity.
Logistic unitManaged transport or storage unit: SSCC.Shipment and handling records linked to recorded contents. A logistic unit and a packaging trade item are different objects even when physically associated.
Party or functionLegal entity or organisational function: GLN.Supplier, recipient, producer and service-provider records. Preserve official identifiers and the role and jurisdiction to which each applies.
LocationPhysical or digital location: GLN.Identify the relevant site or location in records, separately from the responsible legal entity where needed.

See Section 12, “Battery and grouping identification”, for allocation, packaging and battery-host relationships.

22.6 Data-carrier and information-sharing summary ​

The physical QR code provides an entry point; the identifier determines the object; the information service supplies the content. A GS1 Digital Link URI connects these elements, but its syntax does not define the regulatory information set or grant access rights.

For non-passport batteries, Article 13(6)(b) requires access to applicable Article 13(1)–(5) information, the Article 18 declaration of conformity, the Article 52(3) report and the waste-prevention and management information in Article 74(1)(a)–(f). SLI batteries additionally require the information in Article 13(6)(c). The information must be complete, up to date and accurate. Keep the applicability and timing questions in Section 22.7 visible when designing these resources.

MechanismImplementation useControl or limitation
Physical QR codeEncode the battery's GS1 Digital Link URI and test that it reaches the correct information at the intended granularity.Article 13(7) requires visible, legible and indelible printing or engraving on the battery. Where this is not possible or warranted on account of its nature and size, affix the labels and QR code to the packaging and accompanying documents. Apply Annex VI, Part C.
Web information serviceMaintain the association between the identifier and the applicable public resources; use a governed linking or resolver service where several resources are needed. Describe the passport with link type gs1:dpp and the non-passport information page with gs1:pip (Section 13.8.1).URI syntax alone does not establish resolver conformance, resource completeness or long-term operation. See Section 13.8, “Resolver and linking to a web resource”.
Passport serviceMaintain the individual record, shared model information and the applicable Annex XIII access layers.Articles 77(2), (4)–(6) and 78 require more than a public web page: structured interoperable information, controlled access, integrity, security and continuity.
Transaction recordsPreserve supplier, recipient, model and quantity relationships needed to answer authority requests.Article 46 records remain necessary independently of a passport or public QR resource.
EPCIS exchangeCapture and query observations about identified batteries and logistic units, linked to party, location and transaction data.Define data quality, correction, access and retention rules. Event sharing does not make all commercial data public.
Master-data exchangeShare relatively stable battery attributes with trading partners using a verified mapping, including GDSN where selected and supported.Keep battery and host-product attributes distinct; do not assume master data includes all passport, transaction or individual lifecycle information.

Physical labels and CE marking remain subject to their own requirements (Article 13(1)–(5); Articles 19–20). Providing digital access to their information does not replace the required physical markings. See Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”.

22.7 Limitations and matters requiring verification ​

The summary is usable for selecting identification and information-sharing functions, but unresolved source and implementation questions prevent it from being treated as a fully verified deployment specification.

MatterConsequence for implementationOpen question
Labelling specifications and application triggersDo not treat 18 August 2026 alone as the unconditional start date for Article 13(1)–(3); retain the later-of rule involving the Article 13(10) act.OQ-REG-001.
QR access where no due-diligence report is requiredDo not create an Article 52(3) report obligation for an excluded operator merely because Article 13(6)(b) references it. The appropriate QR presentation remains unresolved.OQ-REG-003.
SLI recycled-content timingVerify what the QR resource must show before the relevant Article 8 disclosure obligation applies.OQ-REG-006.
Circular operations outside passport scopeVerify the Article 13(9) status-change fields and required granularity without automatically applying full Article 77 passport architecture.OQ-REG-007.
Passport interoperability and access implementationCheck applicable secondary acts, access entitlements, identifier-system compatibility and continuity arrangements against Articles 77–78. A GS1 URI is only one component.OQ-REG-017; the uncoded interoperability question in Section 29.6, “Open legal and technical questions”.
Recycling and national reportingConfirm evidence and national submission requirements. GS1 records supply inputs rather than statutory results.Section 15.9; OQ-REG-014.

Implementation also depends on valid identifier allocation, accurate source data, maintained battery-host and logistic-content relationships, and a named owner for each information resource. These controls determine whether the selected GS1 standards deliver useful interoperability throughout the required period.
