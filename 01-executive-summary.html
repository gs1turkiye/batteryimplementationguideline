1. Executive summary ​

1.1 Background ​

Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 introduces a common EU framework for batteries and waste batteries, replacing the previous Batteries Directive. It is part of the EU’s wider move towards a more circular and sustainable economy and covers the full battery lifecycle, from design, production and placing on the market to use, reuse, repurposing, collection and recycling.

The Regulation applies to all battery categories and sets requirements for areas such as sustainability, safety, labelling, carbon footprint, recycled content, due diligence, producer responsibility and end-of-life management. It also introduces new information-access requirements, including QR-code access to information and, for selected battery categories, a digital battery passport from 18 February 2027.

In practice, these requirements mean that organisations need to manage reliable data about batteries, the actors involved and the events that occur across the value chain. This creates a need for common identifiers, clear data relationships and interoperable ways to access and exchange information over time. GS1 standards support the implementation of the Regulation by providing a shared global language for identifying batteries, locations and companies, and for linking the right information to the right object throughout its lifecycle.

1.2 Purpose of the guideline ​

Implementing the Battery Regulation requires organisations to connect relevant information to the batteries, activities and parties it concerns. This guideline explains how GS1 standards support that work across all battery categories, including batteries both within and outside the mandatory battery-passport scope.

The guideline takes the requirements of the Battery Regulation as its starting point is the regulatory function: what information an economic operator needs to provide, which battery the information relates to and who needs access to it. It then shows how GS1 standards support a scalable and interoperable implementation by providing globally recognised identifiers, standardised data carriers and structured data-exchange mechanisms. Together, these standards help organisations connect regulatory information to the right battery, operator, location or lifecycle event, enabling more consistent compliance processes, more efficient collaboration across the value chain and better access to trusted information for authorised users and digital services.

1.3 Batteries covered ​

This guideline applies to all battery categories within Regulation (EU) 2023/1542: • portable batteries including portable batteries of general use; • light means of transport (LMT) batteries; • starting, lighting and ignition (SLI) batteries; • industrial batteries; and electric vehicle (EV) batteries.

It also covers batteries incorporated in or supplied with products, relevant configurations and circular operations such as reuse, repurposing, remanufacturing and recycling, are also addressed.

From 18 February 2027, a battery passport will be required for LMT batteries, industrial batteries above 2 kWh and h EV batteries placed on the market. Portable batteries, SLI batteries and industrial batteries with a capacity of 2 kWh or less are outside that passport scope, but may still be subject to QR-code information requirements depending on the battery category and information concerned Where the category or relevant capacity is unclear, this should be determined before selecting the appropriate implementation approach.

1.4 Who should use the guideline ​

This guideline is intended for organisations that place batteries on the EU market or support those activities, including manufacturers, importers, distributors, producers and other professional operators within these organisations, it is particularly relevant for product, compliance, supply-chain, sustainability and IT teams involved in battery data, identification, labelling and data sharing. Service providers may also use the guideline to support customer implementation.

Readers should begin by identifying the relevant battery category, and the roles their organisation performs under the regulation. Category-specific sections explain the relevant requirements and guidance that apply to each battery category while the common sections provide shared legal and technical guidance.

1.5 Regulatory context and role of GS1 standards ​

Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 defines the legal obligations for batteries. This guideline does not interpret or replace those obligations; it explains how GS1 standards support practical implementation where identification, the use of QR and information exchange are required.

The guideline connects key regulatory functions, such a product information, conformity, labelling and QR-code access, traceability, due diligence, producer responsibility, waste management and circular operations to GS1 standards and implementation considerations. Organisations must also consider applicable amendments and secondary legislation when determining their specific requirements.

1.6 How GS1 standards support implementation ​

GS1 standards support organisations in the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 by providing a globally consistent way to identify batteries, parties, locations and logistic units. They also provide standardised methods for carrying these identifiers in QR codes and for enabling access to, and exchange of, related data across the value chain. Used appropriately, these standards help organisations connect regulatory information to the correct battery, operator, location or lifecycle event, making that information easier to access, use and exchange among trading partners, authorised users and digital services. The Regulation remains the source of the legal obligations, data requirements and responsibilities, while GS1 standards provide implementation components that support compliance processes.

GS1 identifiers help organisations to connect battery information and records to the appropriate identification level. A GTIN identifies a battery as a trade item, a GTIN plus batch/lot number distinguishes a production batch and a GTIN plus serial number identifies an individual battery instance. This allows battery model information batch-specific records or battery instance to remain clearly separated but connected where needed

Information access builds on that identification. A GS1 Digital Link URI then provides a standard way to connect an identified battery to relevant online information through a QR-code.based on, information need and access rights. Depending on battery category and applicable legal requirements, this may support access to a battery passport or to other required information for batteries outside the passport scope.

Across the supply chain, GS1 standards also identify economic operatos and other parties, locations and logistic units exchange of visibility data for processes such as shipping, receiving or transformation. Master data exchange further simplifies implementation by allowing stable attributes, which do not change often, to be shared once and reused across processes. This helps organisations maintain traceability and connect information to the relevant batteries and lifecycle activities.

GS1 provides a toolbox of interoperable standards that help companies identify batteries, parties, locations and logistic units, capture relevant lifecycle events and share trusted data consistently across systems and trading partners. By creating reliable links between the right battery, actor, activity and information, these standards make it easier to organise regulatory data and support practical implementation of the Battery Regulation.

1.7 GS1 standards in regulatory compliance ​

GS1 standards support implementation of the Battery Regulation, but they are not mandated by the Regulation and do not on their own demonstrate compliance. They do not replace legal obligation, conformity assessment, testing, verification, certification, audit or decisions by competent authorities.

Where official registration numbers are required, GS1 identifiers reference or connect to them but do not replace them. Similarly, a QR-code or GS1 Digital Link provides access to information, but is not itself a battery passport. Responsibility for the required information, evidence and processes remains with the relevant economic operators.
