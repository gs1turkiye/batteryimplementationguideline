5. Portable batteries ​

5.1 Battery passport necessity ​

A portable battery does not require a battery passport under Article 77(1). From 18 February 2027, the Article 13(6)(b) QR-code route applies.

If classification is uncertain, or a battery is repurposed for a different intended use, classification must be resolved before relying on this result. Further details on determining battery-passport applicability and implementing the relevant information requirements are provided in Section 3.4, “Determine whether a battery passport is required”, and Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”.

5.2.1 Batteries incorporated in products ​

From 18 February 2027, a person placing on the market a product incorporating a portable battery must ensure that the battery is readily removable and replaceable by the end-user throughout the product lifetime (Article 11(1); Article 96(2)(a)). The rule concerns the entire battery, not individual cells or other parts. Readily removable means removable with commercially available tools, without specialised tools unless supplied free with the product, proprietary tools, thermal energy or solvents.

The product must include instructions and safety information on battery use, removal and replacement, permanently available on a public website in a form easily understood by end-users.

The rule is subject to Article 11(2)–(3) derogations and exclusions. Replacement batteries must be available as spare parts for at least five years after the last equipment-model unit is placed on the market, at a reasonable and non-discriminatory price; software must not impede replacement with a compatible battery or key components (Article 11(7)–(8)). The derogations are listed in Section 10.6, “Removability and replaceability”, as replaced by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1770. Current guidance must be checked (OQ-REG-004).

The battery and host product are separate objects. Master data must state which object each attribute describes and maintain their relationship.

5.3 Additional requirements for portable batteries of general use ​

A portable battery of general use is a portable-battery subcategory. It comprises batteries specifically designed to be interoperable and having one of the formats listed in Article 3(1), point (10): 4.5 V (3R12), button cell, D, C, AA, AAA, AAAA, A23 or 9 V (PP3). The definition covers rechargeable and non-rechargeable batteries. The interoperable design and listed format are cumulative elements; a battery is not within the subcategory merely because it is portable or has a similar use.

Portable batteries of general use have the same Article 77(1) passport outcome and Article 13(6)(b) QR-code route as other portable batteries. Their material differences are the Article 9 performance and durability requirements and separate reporting under Article 75(1)(b).

5.3.1 Performance and durability ​

From 18 August 2028 or 24 months after the Article 9(2) delegated act enters into force, whichever is later, manufacturers must ensure that portable batteries of general use other than button cells meet the minimum values established by that act for the applicable Annex III electrochemical performance and durability parameters (Article 9(1)–(2)).

Battery typeAnnex III parameters
Non-rechargeable (Part A)Minimum average duration, delayed discharge performance and resistance to leakage
Rechargeable (Part B)Rated capacity, charge (capacity) retention, charge (capacity) recovery, endurance in cycles and resistance to leakage
Button cellExcluded from the minimum-value requirements in Article 9(1)–(2)

The Article 9(2) delegated act has not been reviewed for this guideline (OQ-REG-015). Its minimum values, measurement conditions and resulting application date must therefore be verified before operational requirements are established.

Article 9(3) requires the Commission to assess, by 31 December 2030, measures concerning non-rechargeable portable batteries of general use. It does not itself prohibit or phase out those batteries.

5.3.2 Separate reporting ​

Producers must separately report the quantity of portable batteries of general use made available for the first time in a Member State, in addition to the reporting applicable to portable batteries generally (Article 75(1)(b)). The first reporting year is 2026, with reports due by 30 June 2027. See Section 15.9, “Reporting quantities placed on the market”, for the calculation and for national reporting formats.

The separate reporting requirement does not create a separate collection-rate target for this subcategory. Article 94(2)(e) instead requires the Commission’s review to consider whether such a target should be introduced. Except for Article 9 and the separate reporting identified above, portable batteries of general use remain subject to the portable-battery requirements in this section.

Where affixing an Article 13 label or QR code to the battery is impossible or unwarranted because of its nature and size, it must be placed on the packaging and accompanying documents (Article 13(7)). This differs from the special packaging rule where the separate-collection symbol would be smaller than 0.47 × 0.47 cm (Article 13(4)). One QR code does not replace other physical markings required by law.

The QR information must be complete, current and accurate. Where the operator or battery is outside Article 52(3), the Regulation does not clearly state how the QR reference to that report is handled; no report duty should be invented (OQ-REG-003).

Manufacturers must carry out the applicable conformity assessment, prepare technical documentation and the EU declaration of conformity, and affix CE marking before market placement or putting into service (Articles 17–20 and 38(2)–(4)). CE marking is a conformity mark, not a battery identifier, and QR access to the declaration does not replace physical CE marking. Further details on conformity assessment and economic-operator obligations are provided in Section 11.

5.5 Due diligence, producer responsibility and waste ​

Battery due diligence is not triggered by passport necessity. It applies only where the economic operator is within Article 47 and is then governed by Articles 48–52. Its application date and further details are provided in Section 14.1, “Scope and applicable economic operators”.

A producer must register in each Member State in which it first makes a portable battery available and may not supply there unless registered; this includes batteries incorporated in products (Article 55(1)–(3) and (12)). A GLN does not replace the authority-issued registration number.

Producers or appointed producer responsibility organisations must establish territory-wide, free collection arrangements for waste portable batteries (Article 59(1)–(2), (4)–(6)). They must attain and durably maintain collection rates of 63% by 31 December 2027 and 73% by 31 December 2030, calculated under Annex XI (Article 59(3); Annex XI). The Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts by 18 August 2027 amending the Annex XI method and adapting the target accordingly (Article 59(7)), so later amendments must be checked (OQ-REG-013).

Distributors must take back qualifying waste portable batteries free of charge and without a new purchase, subject to Article 62(1)–(5). Producers or their organisations provide Article 74(1) end-user information; producers provide model-specific information required by Article 74(2)–(3).

Annual reporting covers, by chemistry and category, amounts first made available and collected, collection rate and destinations; it includes batteries incorporated in products and separately reports the portable batteries of general use amount (Article 75(1), (4), (7)–(9)). The first reporting year is 2026, with reports due by 30 June 2027, and formats are set nationally (Section 15.9). Further details on producer responsibility, waste collection and reporting are provided in Sections 15 and 16.

Following preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing, the operator placing the battery on the market or putting it into service is treated as manufacturer (Articles 38(11) and 45). Article 13(9) requires new labels and QR-accessible status-change information, but exact non-passport granularity remains unresolved (OQ-REG-007). Further details on obligations following circular operations are provided in Section 17.

The Regulation requires identification, information-access and traceability functions but does not prescribe GS1 standards.

Granularity follows the function. High-volume portable batteries will commonly use trade-item/model and batch levels; the QR duty does not itself create individual serialisation. Where a battery is incorporated in or supplied with another trade item, the battery GTIN, host-product GTIN and relationship should be separate. GDSN supports master-data exchange only where relevant attributes and target-market implementation are verified.

For portable batteries of general use, the implementation must distinguish the individual battery format, a saleable multipack, any higher packaging level and the production batch. An identifier assigned to a multipack does not identify each battery contained in it.

5.7 Example: rechargeable AA batteries in a consumer pack ​

Rechargeable AA batteries specifically designed to be interoperable are portable batteries of general use under Article 3(1), point (10). They do not require a battery passport under Article 77(1), and the Article 13(6)(b) QR-code route applies from 18 February 2027.

Because the batteries are rechargeable, the capacity label applies from the conditional date in Article 13(2) and (10), while Article 13(3) does not apply. The batteries become subject to the minimum values for the rechargeable Annex III parameters from the conditional application date in Article 9(1)–(2).

GTIN identifies the saleable consumer pack as a trade item. If an individual battery is also managed as a separate trade item, it has a separate GTIN. The pack GTIN does not identify each contained battery. GTIN plus batch or lot number associates the batteries with production and conformity records. A GS1 Digital Link URI supports access to information associated with the identified trade item, but Article 13 governs the legally required content and placement.

The producer must include the batteries in portable-battery collection and reporting and separately report the amount of portable batteries of general use first made available under Article 75(1)(b).

5.9 Limitations and open questions ​

This section does not reproduce the classification tests in Section 4 or extend Article 9 to every portable battery. Button cells are within the portable-battery-of-general-use definition but excluded from Article 9(1)–(2). The portable-battery collection targets apply to the category collectively; they are not separate targets for portable batteries of general use.

Current-law checks remain necessary for Article 13 specifications/date (OQ-REG-001), Cd/Pb transition (OQ-REG-002), QR access where no Article 52(3) report exists (OQ-REG-003), Article 11 guidance/derogations (OQ-REG-004), circular-operation QR information (OQ-REG-007), national reporting formats (Section 15.9) and collection methodology (OQ-REG-013). GS1 standards support implementation but do not establish compliance.
