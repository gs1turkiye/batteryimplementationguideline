16. Waste collection, treatment and recycling ​

This section applies to waste batteries in every battery category, whether or not the battery was subject to a mandatory battery passport. Chapter VIII establishes category-specific collection arrangements and common treatment, recycling and reporting requirements. For these applications GS1 identifiers or EPCIS can support interoperable.

16.1 Category-specific collection systems ​

Producers, or producer responsibility organisations where appointed, are responsible for arrangements covering batteries regardless of nature, chemistry, condition, brand or origin within the category.

CategoryRequired actionRegulatory source
Portable, including general-useFree, territory-wide separate collection through connected points and delivery to a permitted treatment facility.Article 59(1), (2), (4)–(6)
LMTFree, territory-wide separate collection with suitable infrastructure and delivery to permitted facilities.Article 60(1), (2), (4)–(7)
SLIFree take-back and separate collection throughout the territory.Article 61(1)–(4)
Industrial, including stationary battery energy storage systemsIrrespective of capacity, private, non-commercial users must not bear dismantling and collection costs where prior dismantling is required at their premises.Article 61(1)–(4)
Electric VehicleFree take-back and separate collection throughout the territory.Article 61(1)–(4)

Distributors must take back qualifying waste batteries free of charge without requiring a purchase. The duty is limited to categories that the distributor has or had in its offer and excludes waste products containing batteries. Distance sellers must provide sufficient collection points; delivery sales require a free-return offer and information when ordering (Article 62(1)–(5)). End-users must discard waste batteries separately from other waste, including mixed municipal waste (Article 64(1) and (2)).

16.2 Collection targets ​

CategoryMinimum collection rateRegulatory Source
Portable45% by 31 December 2023; 63% by 31 December 2027; 73% by 31 December 2030Article 59(3); Annex XI

No percentage targets exist for SLI, industrial or EV batteries. Teh Regulation divides weight collected in a year by average annual weight first made available to end-users during the preceding three years, calculated separately for portable and LMT batteries. The Commission may amend the methods and targets (Article 59(7); Article 60(8)); current acts must be checked before configuring calculations.

16.3 End-user information ​

Producers or appointed producer responsibility organisations must provide information on waste prevention and extending use, separateand available collection, preparation and treatment routes, safe handling, including lithium risks, label meanings, and impacts of substances and inappropriate disposal (Article 74(1)(a)–(f), (4) and (7)). The information must be provided regularly for each model from first supply, visibly at sale and online, in an understandable language determined by the Member State. Distributors must display this information and explain free return, when simultaneously commercial confidentiality must be preserved.

16.4 Handover and transaction records ​

End-of-life vehicle and WEEE treatment-facility operators must hand removed batteries to the relevant producer, producer responsibility organisation or selected waste operator for treatment and keep transaction records.

An operational record may associate date, source, destination, parties, category, chemistry, measured quantity, logistic-unit reference and intended operation. These are implementation data, not invented legal requirements. GLN can be used to identify parties, functions or locations. SSCC can be used to identify the logistic unit (i.e. trade items packed on a pallet or in a container), but should not be used to identify each battery inside.

16.5 Storage and transport ​

Collection infrastructure must suit expected volume and hazardous nature and meet applicable safety rules according to regulation. Portable and LMT transport containers must meet Directive 2008/68/EC requirements (Article 59(1)(c); Article 60(1)(c)). At treatment facilities, impermeable surfaces and weatherproof covering or suitable containers are required; batteries must not be mixed with conductive or combustible waste (Annex XII, Part A, points 2 and 3). Lithium-based batteries require heat, water, damage, orientation, ventilation, isolation and warning controls in Annex XII, Part A, point 4.

Producers must give relevant operators safety information and, from first supply, provide relevant waste operators electronically, free and on request, with model-specific dismantling, storage, transport and treatment information, including components, materials and hazardous-substance locations. GS1 identifiers can link to controlled information as dangerous-goods classification or transport compliance.

16.6 Treatment requirements ​

Separately collected batteries must undergo treatment and not be disposed of or sent to energy recovery. Treatment must occur in a permitted facility, comply with Annex XII, Part A and use best available techniques under Directive 2010/75/EU. It must remove fluids and acids. Mercury and cadmium must each be separately collected into identifiable streams. Batteries must be removed from WEEE, end-of-life vehicles and other waste products during treatment.

An implementation can use different hierarchies of GS1 identification structures for batteries. GTIN identifies a battery model or trade item; GTIN plus batch or lot number identifies a batch; GTIN plus serial number identifies an instance.

16.7 Recycling efficiency and material recovery ​

Recyclers must accept all waste batteries made available for preparation or recycling, subject to applicable Union law (Article 71(1)). Targets are (Article 71(2) and (3); Annex XII, Parts B and C):

DateTarget by material
Efficiency by 31 December 2025Lead-acid 75%; lithium-based 65%; nickel-cadmium 80%; other 50% by average weight
Efficiency by 31 December 2030Lead-acid 80%; lithium-based 70% by average weight
Recovery by 31 December 2027Cobalt, copper, lead and nickel 90% each; lithium 50%
Recovery by 31 December 2031Cobalt, copper, lead and nickel 95% each; lithium 80%

Article 71(4) requires calculation, verification and documentation rules. Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/606 is listed as a source gap but is not stored locally. Calculation boundaries, allocation, evidence and templates must be verified against the authentic act.

Reporting covers every recycling step and output fraction. In multi-facility recycling, the first recycler collects and reports the information. The reports are due within six months; the first period is the first full calendar year after entry into force of Article 76(5) . Authorities have to establish electronic systems and formats (Article 75(8) and (9)). Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2289 is a source gap; its authentic text is needed to confirm the first reporting year and formats. GS1 identifiers can support source records.

16.10 How EPCIS may support collection and treatment events ​

An implementation can use EPCIS for events concerning identified batteries, batches, logistic units, parties and locations. EPCIS captures those events. ObjectEvent records observations or status changes, while AggregationEvents associates those objects with a parent logistic unit. TransformationEvent are necessary to expresses recycling inputs and outputs. GS1 Core Business Vocabulary (CBV) defines terms, definitions and context such as collecting, decommissioning, disassembling and inspecting to be used to store events.

16.11 Mixed and damaged battery streams ​

Collection covers batteries regardless of condition, while infrastructure and frequency must reflect volume and hazardous nature (Articles 59(1), 60(1) and (4), and 61(1) and (3)). Annex XII adds lithium precautions and prohibits mixing with conductive or combustible waste.

A mixed container may be identified by SSCC and associated with measured or estimated content by category and chemistry. After sorting, new logistic units and measured contents can be related to the incoming unit. Damaged, leaking or thermally affected batteries must be segregated and recorded at the level needed for safety and transport controls. If an original label, serial number, QR code or passport identifier is unreadable, an operator must not create an unverified link. A local consignment identifier may preserve custody while recording that original identity is unknown.
