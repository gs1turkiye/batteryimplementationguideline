10. Product, sustainability and safety requirements ​

10.1 How to use this section ​

This section summarises the sustainability, safety, labelling and information requirements that apply when batteries are placed on the market or put into service under the Regulation. A battery may be placed on the market or put into service only if it meets those applicable requirements and does not present a risk to human health, the safety of persons, property or the environment.

The outcome depends on category, capacity, rechargeability, chemistry, configuration, date and, in some cases, prior circular operations.

10.2 Restrictions on substances ​

Batteries must not contain substances restricted by Annex I unless the relevant conditions are met. This applies across battery categories and operates in addition to Annex XVII to Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 and Article 4(2)(a) of Directive 2000/53/EC. The current Annex I must be checked for the substance, battery and use concerned; a general assertion of “substance compliance” is not a substitute (Article 6).

10.3 Carbon-footprint requirements ​

Carbon-footprint requirements apply to EV batteries, LMT batteries and rechargeable industrial batteries above 2 kWh. They do not currently apply to portable batteries, SLI batteries or industrial batteries at or below 2 kWh. Rechargeable industrial batteries with exclusively external storage have later dates (Article 7).

Each requirement depends on delegated or implementing acts. A base date alone is insufficient: the duty starts on that date or after the specified period following entry into force of the relevant act, whichever is latest. Current methods, formats, classes and thresholds must be verified.

The legal granularity is model per manufacturing plant, not automatically individual instance. Batch or serial data support traceability. The carbon-footprint requirements do not apply where a battery was already placed on the market or put into service before preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing. This does not cover a newly manufactured battery or one first placed on the market only after the operation (Article 7(5)).

10.4 Recycled-content requirements ​

Recycled-content requirements apply to industrial batteries above 2 kWh, EV batteries, LMT batteries and SLI batteries where cobalt, lead, lithium or nickel is present within their material scope. Industrial batteries with exclusively external storage are excluded from the documentation and minimum-share requirement. The duty is conditional on category and material content (Article 8(1)–(3)).

The first documentation date is 18 August 2028 or 24 months after entry into force of the methodology-and-format delegated act, whichever is latest. Targets, materials and shares may be amended or supplemented, so current law must be checked (Article 8(5)–(6)).

Article 8 sets model–year–plant granularity. The model, year and plant information must be retained. Instance access should resolve to the correct model–year–plant record. A battery with a battery passport has an individual identifier, but that does not make an individual recycled-content calculation necessary. Article 8(1)–(3) does not apply to batteries that have been subject to preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing if they had already been placed on the market or put into service before undergoing such operations (Article 8(4)).

10.5 Performance and durability ​

The Regulation establishes distinct category-specific performance and durability requirements (Articles 9–10).

10.5.1 Portable batteries of general use ​

Portable batteries of general use, excluding button cells, must meet minimum values for the Annex III parameters. This applies from 18 August 2028 or 24 months after the relevant delegated act enters into force, whichever is latest. Until that act is verified, mandatory values and the operative date cannot be supplied.

10.6 Removability and replaceability ​

From 18 February 2027, the responsible actor is any natural or legal person placing on the market a product incorporating the battery; this is not only a battery-manufacturer duty (Articles 11 and 96(2)(a)).

For products incorporating portable batteries, the entire battery must be readily removable and replaceable by the end-user throughout the product lifetime. For end-user removability and replaceability duty, the obligation applies only to the entire portable battery, not to individual cells or other parts contained within it. Commercially available tools are permitted, but not specialised tools unless supplied free, proprietary tools, thermal energy or solvents. The product must have instructions and safety information on battery use, removal and replacement, also permanently available online on a public website in an easily understandable form.

The qualifications and related duties must be preserved:

  • professional-only removal is permitted for the products in the derogation table below, in most cases only where required for user and appliance safety;
  • the end-user removability and replaceability requirement does not apply where continuity of power supply is necessary and a permanent connection between the product and the battery is required for the safety of the user and the appliance or, for products whose main function is collecting and supplying data, for data integrity (Article 11(3));
  • products incorporating LMT batteries must permit the battery and individual cells in its pack to be removed and replaced by an independent professional;
  • a replacement must be compatible without affecting functioning, performance or safety;
  • portable and LMT batteries must remain available as spare parts at a reasonable and non-discriminatory price for at least five years after the last unit of the equipment model is placed on the market; and
  • software must not impede replacement with a compatible battery or key components.

Article 11(9) requires the Commission to publish guidelines to facilitate the harmonised application of Article 11. Their current status is part of OQ-REG-004.

The following products incorporating portable batteries may be designed so that the battery is removable and replaceable only by independent professionals. Article 11(2) is shown as replaced by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1770, Article 1 (official text), which enters into force on 11 November 2026. Article 11 itself applies from 18 February 2027.

10.7 Safety of stationary battery energy storage systems ​

The safety requirements apply irrespective of whether system capacity exceeds the battery-passport threshold. (Articles 12 and 77(1)).

Stationary battery energy storage systems must be safe during normal operation and use. From 18 August 2024, technical documentation must demonstrate compliance and successful testing for applicable parameters using state-of-the-art methods (Annex V); assess hazards not addressed by Annex V; evidence successful mitigation and testing; and include mitigation instructions for hazards that could occur, such as fire or explosion. A parameter applies only where the corresponding hazard exists under conditions envisaged by the manufacturer. Documentation must be reviewed after preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing.

10.8 State of health and expected lifetime ​

For stationary battery energy storage systems, LMT batteries and EV batteries, battery management systems must contain up-to-date parameter data from 18 August 2024 as stated in Annex VII. This does not extend to every industrial battery above 2 kWh (Article 14).

Read-only access must be provided, respecting manufacturer intellectual property rights and on a non-discriminatory basis, to the person who legally purchased the battery—including an independent operator or waste management operator—or a third party acting for that person. Access is limited to energy-storage participation; evaluation of residual value, remaining lifetime or further-use capability; and facilitation of listed circular operations.

The system must include a software reset function where a circular operator needs different software. If used, the original manufacturer is not liable for a breach attributable to software uploaded after placement on the market. For EV batteries, these requirements apply in addition to Union vehicle type-approval law.

10.11 Consolidated GS1 support and granularity ​

GS1 standards support companies in implementing the Regulation by providing a consistent way to identify batteries, economic operators, production plants, and related entities, as well as to capture and share relevant data.

Requirement areaHow GS1 standards support the functionGranularity and limitations
Restrictions on substancesA GTIN identifies the trade-item model associated with a controlled material declaration. GTIN plus batch or lot number associates production evidence with a batch, while GTIN plus serial number supports an instance investigation. A GLN identifies a manufacturer, testing function or manufacturing location. EPCIS shares material-receipt, production, release, recall and corrective-action events.Use model identification for an unchanged specification and batch identification where inputs or test results vary. These standards do not validate composition or establish conformity. A GLN does not replace a legally required identity, accreditation or authority-issued identifier.
Carbon footprintA GTIN combined with a batch or lot number provides access to the carbon footprint declaration provided there is a link between that batch or lot to a single manufacturing plant. A GLN identifies the manufacturing plant to enable the link to the manufacturing plant. EPCIS shares data about carbon footprint. GS1 Digital Link encoded in a QR helps parties in the supply chain to access relevant information.Although the regulation does not explicitly require a batch number, it requires that the data associated with the record must distinguish between the manufacturing plant. For this reason, using a GTIN together with a batch/lot number is recommended. This approach helps ensure that the relevant carbon footprint record can be clearly linked to the specific battery model, manufacturing plant and production batch.
Recycled contentAt a minimum, a GTIN combined with a batch/lot number enables companies to document and track levels of recycled content as long as the batch is made up of same amount of recycled content. Where instance level identification is applied for example when there is a requirement for a battery passport, a GTIN and serial number identify the instance. GTIN and batch/lot identify the content used. A GLN identifies the manufacturing plant to enable the link to the manufacturing plant. EPCIS shares data about recycled content. GS1 Digital Link encoded in a QR code helps parties in the supply chain to access relevant information such as model, year and plant.The Regulation requires companies to track, document, and eventually meet minimum levels of recycled cobalt, lead, lithium, and nickel in the battery types in scope. Since the information must be specific enough to distinguish between battery models, production years, and manufacturing plants, it is recommended to use at least a GTIN together with a batch/lot number. For batteries that require a product passport and instance-level identification, a GTIN together with a serial number also gives access to the relevant information for the specific model–year–plant record. Identification of raw materials makes it easier to keep track of the recycled content.
Performance and durabilityA GTIN identifies the model to which a performance document applies. Master data associated with that GTIN holds the performance and durability information. A GS1 Digital Link URI enables access to the battery performance and durability information.The regulation requires performance and durability information such as how long the battery lasts, how well it performs after storage, how many times it can be recharged, where relevant, and whether it is resistant to leakage. As this information should be common at a model level A GTIN is sufficient. GDSN exchanges trade item master data only where attribute coverage has been verified (see Section 20.11, “GDSN and master-data exchange”). No published GS1 Web Vocabulary term for these data points was found.
Removability and replaceabilityGTIN to identify the model. A separate GTIN to identify the host product. A GS1 Digital Link URI enables access to instructions and safety information on the use, removal and replacement of the batteries and a GLN identifies a party or service location. A GTIN plus serial number keeps track of replacement and repair of batteries.A battery GTIN identifies the battery trade item but does not, by itself, identify the host model in which the battery is incorporated or record when the last unit of that host model was placed on the market. The person placing the product on the market must ensure that the instructions and safety information on use, removal and replacement are permanently available online on a publicly available website (Article 11(1)). The instruction resource therefore needs to remain accessible after the ordinary commercial product page has been withdrawn.
Safety of stationary battery energy storage systemsMaster data relating to the model share safety information and instructions. A GS1 Digital Link URI enables access to safety information and instructions for the system. A GTIN plus serial number enables parties to review if a battery is prepared for re-use, prepared for repurposing, remanufactured or repurposed. EPCIS shares commissioning, inspection, maintenance or circular-operation events.Identification at model level is sufficient to provide information about the safety instructions. Article 12 applies to every stationary battery energy storage system, whatever its capacity, while a battery passport is required only above 2 kWh (Article 77(1)). Where a system uses GTIN plus serial number for instance-level identification, this also provides technical documentation at instance level, making it possible to review information if a battery is prepared for re-use, prepared for repurposing, remanufactured or repurposed. At or below 2 kWh, instance-level identification is an implementation choice.
State of health and expected lifetimeA GTIN gives access to information about safety, tests and instructions for a battery model. However, a GTIN plus serial number will make it possible to track and provide state of the health information if the battery is re-used, prepared for repurposing, remanufactured or repurposed. A GS1 Digital Link URI provides an entry point to access-controlled resources, and EPCIS supports lifecycle events.Identification at model level is sufficient to provide generic information about the battery safety tests, instructions. Because condition changes over time, instance identification is normally required; model master data are insufficient. Battery manufacturers must ensure that certain battery data related to state of health and expected lifetime—can be accessed through the battery management system on a read-only basis. This access must be provided fairly and without discrimination to the legal battery owner, independent operators, waste management operators, or authorised third parties, while still respecting the manufacturer’s intellectual property rights. Verifiable credentials structured using the GS1 Verifiable Credentials Data Model may support machine-verifiable assertions about a party’s identity, role or delegated authority. An access-control system may use those assertions when deciding whether access should be granted. Verifiable credentials do not themselves enforce access rights, and implementation depends on credential issuance, trust, revocation, authentication and authorisation governance.

Further details on identifier allocation and granularity are provided in Section 12, “Battery and grouping identification”, and on data-sharing implementation in Section 20, “Supply-chain traceability and information exchange”.
