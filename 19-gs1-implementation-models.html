19. GS1 implementation models ​

This section brings battery identification, information access and data exchange together into practical implementation models. An operator selects a product configuration, the identification levels needed for its records, and the information services supporting its obligations. Several models can therefore form one implementation: a battery incorporated in a product can have shared model data, individual passport records and supply-chain events.

The applications described here are GS1 approaches proposed by this guideline. The legal requirements cited in each model determine the responsible actor, information and scope; they do not prescribe the GS1 system. Identifier allocation, data ownership, access control and maintenance are implementation responsibilities. GS1 records support evidence management but do not replace testing, conformity assessment, audit, verification or an authority decision.

Passport status follows the battery category and capacity, not the selected commercial configuration or GS1 model. From 18 February 2027, Article 77(1) requires a passport for each LMT battery, industrial battery above 2 kWh and EV battery placed on the market or put into service. Portable batteries, including portable batteries of general use, SLI batteries and industrial batteries at or below 2 kWh do not require that passport but remain subject to Article 13(6)(b), with Article 13(6)(c) additionally applying to SLI batteries. Where category or relevant capacity is unknown, further information is needed before selecting the QR route. These outcomes govern all the cross-category models below.

19.1 Battery sold as a trade item ​

Manufacturers need to connect the identification on the battery with the model and relevant production records (Article 38(6)). Economic operators also need retrievable supply-chain information under Article 46. This model applies to separately supplied batteries in any category; select the passport or non-passport route using the scope above.

GTIN identifies the battery trade item, with batch/lot or serial identification where the function requires it. The manufacturer or other responsible GTIN allocator maintains the trade-item record; trading partners use the same identifiers to match orders, deliveries and associated information.

Implementation stepRecord or relationship to maintainPractical effect
Establish the battery trade item.Battery GTIN linked to the regulatory model, category, relevant capacity and controlled product information.Trading partners can retrieve the information for the battery actually supplied. A GTIN does not encode these attributes.
Identify production at the required level.Battery GTIN plus batch/lot number, or GTIN plus serial number, linked to the model record.Production evidence and individual records remain distinguishable from shared model information.
Describe traded packaging levels.Pack or case GTIN, where required by GS1 allocation rules, linked to the contained battery trade item and quantity.Ordering a pack does not lose the identity or number of batteries it contains.
Capture receipt and supply.Supplier, recipient, quantities and exact battery models, with transaction references and finer identifiers where used.Records are retrievable for Article 46 without relying on the outer packaging remaining available.
Provide QR access.Battery identity linked to the applicable information resource or individual passport.Apply Section 19.7 or 19.8 according to the battery's passport status.

A packaging GTIN and an SSCC serve different purposes: one identifies a trade item, while the other identifies an individual logistic unit. Maintain both relationships where both are used. Further details on trade-item and logistic-unit identification are provided in Section 12.2, “Battery as a trade item”, and Section 12.5, “Logistic-unit identification using SSCC”.

Technical basis: GS1 General Specifications, release 26.0, sections 1.3.1–1.3.1.4, pp. 35–40, and 1.3.5.1, p. 56; GS1 GTIN Management Standard, release 1.1, sections 2.1 and 2.8, pp. 10 and 16–17.

19.2 Battery incorporated in another trade item ​

Incorporation does not remove the battery from the Regulation's scope (Article 1(3)). Battery identification remains subject to Article 38(6), and cell or module suppliers provide manufacturers with information and documentation necessary for compliance under Article 39. This model applies across categories; the battery's own category and capacity determine passport status.

Separate GS1 identities and a maintained relationship between them distinguish the battery from the host product. Where both objects are trade items, the battery GTIN identifies the battery and the host-product GTIN identifies the host. The implementation records which battery model, batch or instance is incorporated.

Information needRelationship to recordBoundary
Describe a product design.Host model linked to permitted battery models, quantities and specification versions.Compatibility with a battery model does not prove that a particular unit contains it.
Identify production affected by a battery batch.Host production batch or instances linked to the installed battery GTIN and batch/lot number.If alternative batteries are used, the relationship needs to distinguish the actual production concerned.
Retrieve an installed battery's individual passport or service history.Identified host instance linked to the battery GTIN plus serial number.The host identity does not substitute for the individual battery identity.
Record removal or replacement.End the previous installation relationship and record the replacement relationship with its effective date and supporting service record.Do not overwrite the history as though the replacement battery had always been installed.

These relationships give access to battery evidence from a host-product record. EPCIS AssociationEvent supports recording installation and removal where its semantics fit; model compatibility data belongs in a separate product relationship record. Further details on recording associations between batteries and host products are provided in Section 20.7, “CTE: Association of batteries with host products”.

Battery chemistry, capacity and mass describe the battery; host-product mass and performance describe the host. If the battery is not managed as a trade item, assess the appropriate identification structure rather than assuming the host GTIN identifies it. The trade-item assumption limits this particular model.

Any host-based access route supplements the applicable battery QR and placement requirements; it does not establish permission to omit the battery marking. Removability and replaceability remain subject to Article 11, including the derogations and exclusion in paragraphs (2)–(3). Technical basis: GS1 General Specifications, sections 1.3.1–1.3.1.4, pp. 35–40; GS1 EPCIS Standard, version 2.0.1, section 7.4.6, pp. 96–102.

19.3 Battery supplied with another trade item ​

A battery supplied with another product remains a distinct regulated object (Article 1(3)). GS1 identification describes the combined sale while retaining the battery identity needed for information access, traceability and reporting. This model applies across categories and does not change the battery's passport outcome.

Maintain a record for the combined trade item, the host product and each battery trade item, stating the quantity of each component. Where the bundle is a predefined assortment, apply the GS1 allocation rules to its composition and changes. For a serialised battery, record which individual battery was packed with the host instance or combined supply instance if the business process needs that relationship.

For example, assume a product is supplied with two batteries already classified as portable batteries of general use. These batteries do not require a passport; Article 13(6)(b) governs their QR information. A combined trade-item record links to the battery GTIN and a quantity of two, while packing records distinguish battery batches if they vary. The battery mass used for battery reporting is the mass of the two batteries, not the combined product's mass.

A contents list identifying two batteries of a model does not identify their individual instances. Nor does an outer commercial QR code automatically satisfy the battery QR placement rule. Further details on batteries supplied with other trade items and QR-code placement are provided in Section 12.4, “Battery supplied with another trade item”, and Section 19.7 below.

Technical basis: GS1 GTIN Management Standard, section 2.9, p. 17; GS1 General Specifications, sections 1.3.1–1.3.1.4, pp. 35–40.

19.4 Model-level implementation ​

Model-level records hold information shared by batteries of the same model. A battery GTIN is the key to this information, supporting model identification and conformity-document retrieval under Article 38(2) and (6). For passport-scope batteries, shared information also supports the model content required by Article 77(2) and Annex XIII. This model is useful across all categories.

Create a controlled relationship between the trade item and the regulatory model; they should not be assumed to have an identical scope in every commercial configuration. Link documents and attributes to their source, responsible owner, version and period of applicability. The record supplies common information to batch records, individual records and web resources without copying uncontrolled versions into each system.

A change in a document does not necessarily require a new GTIN. Assess product changes under the applicable GS1 allocation rules, while retaining the evidence version applicable to batteries already supplied. GDSN supports exchange of verified trade item attributes with trading partners; it is not assumed to contain every regulatory field or document.

Model data alone cannot supply an individual passport or distinguish one battery's usage history. For non-passport batteries, a model-level resource holds shared information, but finer identification and records remain necessary where required by the information concerned. Further details on choosing the appropriate identification level are provided in Section 12.6, “Choosing the appropriate identification level”.

Technical basis: GS1 General Specifications, section 1.3.1, pp. 35–36; GS1 GTIN Management Standard, sections 1–2, pp. 8–18.

19.5 Batch-level implementation ​

Batch identification connects production evidence and affected stock to a defined group of batteries. It supports Article 38(6) identification and, where relevant, corrective action under Articles 38(9), 41(6) and 42(5). The approach applies across categories; it supplements rather than replaces individual identification where a passport is required.

GTIN plus batch/lot number identifies the battery production batch. Maintain the following relationships:

  1. Link the batch to its battery model and relevant production documentation.
  2. Associate batch-specific information with that batch, rather than publishing it as though it applied to every battery carrying the GTIN.
  3. Preserve the batch identity through packing and supply records, recording multiple batches separately where a logistic unit contains a mixture.
  4. Where batteries are serialised, link their individual identities to the batch so that one batch query can retrieve the affected instances.

This supports evidence retrieval and more precise stock searches. It does not prove that every battery in a batch has a measured characteristic unless the underlying evidence supports that conclusion. A batch/lot number without its GTIN is insufficient for this GS1 identification structure.

A GS1 Digital Link URI qualified by batch/lot number connects a scan to batch-specific information; Section 19.7 explains its non-passport use. Technical basis: GS1 General Specifications, section 1.3.1.2, pp. 37–38; GS1 Digital Link Standard: URI Syntax, release 1.6.0, section 5.3, p. 37.

19.6 Instance-level implementation ​

Individual records are necessary for passport-scope batteries under Article 77(1)–(3). They may also serve operational needs such as service history or individual recall handling for non-passport batteries. Voluntary serialisation does not change the latter batteries' legal passport status.

GTIN plus serial number identifies each individual battery. Create the instance record before relying on the identifier in downstream processes, link it to the model and production batch where recorded, and preserve the identity in installation, supply and service records. Validate that the physical identifier retrieves the intended instance rather than only a generic product page.

The individual record refers to shared model evidence while retaining its own observations and changes. A document or event reference should identify the battery concerned and the relevant time or version; replacing an information-service provider should not break that association.

For circular operations, distinguish three decisions: the battery's legal status and responsible operator, the required passport relationship, and the appropriate GS1 identity. Articles 38(11), 45 and 77(7) govern the regulatory consequences. GS1 General Specifications, section 2.1.15, pp. 133–134, governs identification of non-new trade items; the GTIN Management Standard alone is not the rule for every such case. A new passport is not automatically an instruction to assign a new GTIN. Keep predecessor links and resolve the applicable identity treatment before implementing the operation.

Further details on obligations following circular operations are provided in Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”. Technical basis for individual identification: GS1 General Specifications, section 1.3.1.4, pp. 39–40.

19.7 Non-passport QR-code implementation ​

This model serves portable batteries, including portable batteries of general use, SLI batteries and industrial batteries at or below 2 kWh. These batteries do not require an Article 77 passport. From 18 February 2027, manufacturers provide QR access under Article 13(6)(b), with importer and distributor checks under Articles 41(2) and 42(2). SLI batteries additionally require Article 13(6)(c) information.

A GS1 Digital Link URI connects the battery identity to the required resources. Choose model, batch or instance granularity according to the information that needs to be retrieved; the QR duty alone does not universally prescribe batch identification or serialisation.

Resource to make accessibleImplementation relationship
Applicable information under Article 13(1)–(5).Link the battery record to the relevant labelling information, using batch-specific data where the information varies.
EU declaration of conformity under Article 18.Identify the declaration covering the battery and retain its applicability and version.
Report under Article 52(3).Link the applicable operator report to the batteries and reporting period it covers. Do not invent a reporting duty for an excluded operator.
Waste-prevention and management information under Article 74(1)(a)–(f).Provide the complete applicable information set, including relevant collection and safety information, rather than an abbreviated recycling statement.
For SLI batteries, the recovered-material information in Article 13(6)(c).Link the battery to the information calculated in accordance with Article 8, retaining the timing qualification below.

Construct the URI using the selected GS1 identifier and qualifiers, encode it in the QR code, and configure the information service to retrieve the associated resources. Describe the information page with link type gs1:pip (Section 13.8.1). Assign an owner to each resource and check that updates remain applicable to the identified batteries. Article 13(6) requires the information to be complete, up to date and accurate.

Article 13(7) requires the labels and QR code to be printed or engraved visibly, legibly and indelibly on the battery. Where this is not possible or warranted on account of its nature and size, they are affixed to the packaging and accompanying documents. Apply Annex VI, Part C to QR readability. QR access does not replace physical markings or CE marking under Articles 19–20.

The presentation where no Article 52(3) report is required remains open under OQ-REG-003. SLI information before the relevant Article 8 disclosure applies remains open under OQ-REG-006. Article 13(9) information after circular operations requires the further assessment recorded in OQ-REG-007. Further details on QR codes for batteries not requiring a battery passport are provided in Section 13.4, “QR codes for batteries not requiring a battery passport”.

Technical basis: GS1 Digital Link Standard: URI Syntax, release 1.6.0, section 4, pp. 19–36, and sections 5.1, 5.3–5.4, pp. 36–37. URI syntax and the operation of the linked service are separate implementation components.

19.8 Battery-passport implementation ​

This model applies to LMT batteries, EV batteries and industrial batteries above 2 kWh within Article 77(1). The economic operator placing the battery on the market attributes its unique identifier and ensures accurate, complete and current passport information; it may authorise another operator in writing to act on its behalf (Article 77(3)–(4)).

A GS1 implementation uses GTIN plus serial number for the individual battery and a GS1 Digital Link URI for access. The resolver describes the passport with link type gs1:dpp (Section 13.8.1). The passport remains a separately governed electronic record. The operator validates the whole implementation against Article 77(3), including the specified ISO/IEC 15459 standards or their equivalent; a syntactically valid URI alone is insufficient.

19.9 Multi-audience GS1 Digital Link implementation ​

A battery can have public information, restricted technical information and commercial supply-chain records associated with the same identity. GS1 Digital Link with a linking service helps users find the appropriate resources. This model works with both QR routes, but statutory passport access layers apply only to batteries within Article 77.

The URI expresses the battery identity. A resolver associates that identity with several resources; the information service applies the relevant access controls. Resource selection and authorisation are separate functions: selecting a repair-information link does not establish a right to read restricted information.

User or systemResource relationshipImplementation control
Public readerApplicable non-passport information or public passport information.Provide a reliable public entry point for the identified battery.
Repairer or recyclerRelevant technical or dismantling information.For restricted passport fields, verify entitlement under Article 77(2), Annex XIII and applicable access measures. A stated occupation alone is not sufficient.
Authority or notified bodyInformation within its regulatory access rights.Authenticate and authorise access at the relevant service. Preserve the requested battery scope.
Trading partnerCommercial documents or event records shared by agreement.Apply commercial access policies separately from public and passport access.

Where a GS1-conformant resolver is selected, describe resources with appropriate link types and metadata (gs1:dpp for the passport and gs1:pip for the non-passport information page; Section 13.8.1), configure the default route and test resolution at each supported identification level. The technical basis is the GS1-Conformant Resolver Standard, release 1.2.1, sections 2.5–2.6 and 2.14. A direct information page is also possible; use of a GS1 Digital Link URI does not imply use of a conformant resolver.

Maintain domain ownership, resource addresses and responsibility for updating links. Test that a request for an individual passport does not silently resolve only to model information. Further details on multiple audiences and information resources are provided in Section 13.9, “Multiple audiences and information resources”.

19.10 Supply-chain event-data implementation ​

Economic operators need to retrieve supplier identity and the identity of operators supplied, together with quantities and exact models, for the Article 46 availability periods. The duty applies across battery categories, independently of passport status. GS1 identifiers and EPCIS connect these records to observations about movement, packing or processing.

The implementation begins with the questions the operator needs to answer. Identify the battery at model, batch or instance level; identify parties and locations using GLN where selected; and identify managed logistic units using SSCC. Preserve the transaction references and content relationships needed to connect the observations to supplies.

Actual processPossible EPCIS implementationRelationship preserved
Observing receipt or shipment.ObjectEvent with the appropriate business context.Identified objects or quantities, event time, relevant locations and transaction references.
Packing batteries or cases into a logistic unit.AggregationEvent where its physical containment semantics apply.Parent logistic-unit identity and contained identities or quantities.
Installing or removing a battery in a host product.AssociationEvent where its semantics apply.The identified battery's relationship to the host.
Consuming material inputs and producing battery outputs.TransformationEvent.Identified inputs, outputs and their quantities, to the precision supported by the process evidence.

EPCIS defines the event structures; CBV supplies standard vocabulary for their business context. Select values from their full definitions. For example, assembly where components remain recognisable is not automatically a transformation. Technical basis: GS1 EPCIS Standard, version 2.0.1, sections 7.4.2–7.4.6, pp. 78–102; GS1 Core Business Vocabulary Standard, section 7.1.3, pp. 36–43.

The capturing party validates the observations and provides controlled access through the agreed exchange arrangements. Define how parties reconcile partial receipts, returns, incorrect scans and changed contents. Shipping and receipt are separate observations; one does not prove the other. SSCC without its contents cannot establish which batteries were shipped.

Retain the Article 46 information for ten years after receipt and ten years after supply, using the applicable trigger for the records concerned (Article 46(2)). EPCIS supports capture, query and access controls, but does not itself set the operator's retention policy or prove compliance. Further details on EPCIS implementation are provided in Section 20.10, “How EPCIS is used”; technical basis: GS1 EPCIS Standard, sections 8.1–8.2, pp. 103–145.

19.11 Due-diligence evidence implementation ​

Operators within Articles 47–48 need supply-chain controls identifying upstream actors and documentation supporting them (Article 49(1)(d) and (2)). This model is relevant across categories, including batteries outside mandatory passport scope. Assess the Article 47 turnover/group test and circular-operation exclusion before applying it.

The application date is stated in Section 14.1, “Scope and applicable economic operators”.

GS1 identifiers connect suppliers, material flows, production and evidence as follows:

Evidence relationshipImplementation methodBoundary
Supplier and source locations.Link GLN party and location records to required names, addresses and supporting documents.A location identifier does not establish country of origin or identify the supplier legal entity unless allocated for that purpose.
Raw material and material batch.Where the material is a trade item, use GTIN with batch/lot qualification and link the required material description, origin and transaction evidence.Preserve source evidence; the identifier does not certify origin.
Material inputs and battery outputs.Link production records, and EPCIS transformation records where appropriate, to battery model or batch records.A possible input-to-output relationship does not prove the measured percentage or weight in each battery.
Verification and policy coverage.Link reports and policy versions to the suppliers, sites, processes and batteries they actually cover.Retrieval does not replace risk assessment, mitigation, verification or audit.
Public report.Associate the Article 52(3) report with the batteries and reporting period covered.Publish the required report while preserving confidentiality of underlying evidence as applicable.

This arrangement allows an operator to follow a battery record back to the relevant supplier and evidence, or identify production affected by a supplier issue. It does not require every raw-material unit to be serialised, nor does it require confidential evidence to be placed in a public QR resource.

Keep the separate retention triggers for management-system records and supporting due-diligence documentation in Articles 49(1)(c) and 48(3). Further details on record retention and the use of GTIN, batch/lot and GLN are provided in Section 14.10, “Record retention”, and Section 14.11, “How GTIN, batch/lot and GLN support implementation”. Technical basis: GS1 General Specifications, sections 1.3.1.2 and 1.3.3–1.3.4, pp. 37–38 and 49–54; GS1 EPCIS Standard, section 7.4.5, pp. 93–96. GDSN use requires the field verification described in Section 19.13.

19.12 EPR and waste-reporting implementation ​

Producers and the organisations acting within the applicable arrangements need to connect registration, batteries first made available in each Member State and reporting records (Articles 55–57 and 75). Waste-management actors have their own reporting duties; the treatment facilities specified in Article 65(1) retain handover records under Article 65(2). These functions apply across categories and are independent of passport status.

GS1 identifiers help join source records and reconcile totals. A producer GLN links to the legal entity, its role in each Member State and the authority-issued registration number. Battery GTIN records hold the relevant battery category, chemistry and mass. Supply records identify quantities, dates and market context.

The reporting process can then:

  1. Identify the responsible producer and reporting jurisdiction for the activity.
  2. Retrieve the battery data applicable to the supplied model and period.
  3. Derive battery quantities from direct sales or recorded host/bundle contents, retaining the source calculation.
  4. Reconcile returns, corrections and other adjustments under the applicable reporting rules.
  5. Aggregate the results into the prescribed reporting dimensions and retain links back to source records.

For waste operations, GLN identifies parties and sites, while SSCC identifies a logistic unit linked to measured quantities, known contents and handover documents. EPCIS records relevant collection, receipt and processing events. A mixed or damaged stream may lack individual identities; record what is known without generating purported original battery serial numbers.

A shipment is not necessarily a first making available on a Member State market. A host-product sale must not count the same battery twice, and host mass must not substitute for battery mass. For waste measurements, distinguish net battery mass from container or other material mass. Statutory calculations, national submission formats and recycling verification remain separate from the identifier system.

Further details on using GTIN and structured data for reporting, waste collection, treatment and recycling are provided in Section 15.11, “How GTIN and structured data support reporting”, and Section 16, “Waste collection, treatment and recycling”. Technical basis: GS1 General Specifications, sections 1.3.1, 1.3.3–1.3.5.1, pp. 35–36 and 49–56; GS1 EPCIS Standard, sections 7.4.2–7.4.5, pp. 78–96. OQ-REG-014 retains the unresolved handover-detail question. Section 15.9 covers national reporting formats.

19.13 Selecting a proportionate model ​

Select the combination that answers the operator's actual information needs. The following sequence is an implementation method, not a new set of regulatory duties.

DecisionImplementation outcome
Confirm category, capacity where relevant, actor and applicable dates.Record whether a passport is required, a non-passport QR route applies, or further classification information is needed.
Determine the battery's commercial configuration.Use Section 19.1, 19.2 or 19.3 to establish the battery, host, packaging and contents relationships.
Determine the scope of each information item.Combine model, batch and instance records using Sections 19.4–19.6. Do not apply finer granularity without a regulatory or operational reason.
Select the information-access route.Use Section 19.7 or 19.8, with Section 19.9 where multiple resources and audiences need to be served.
Identify the additional record functions.Add the event, due-diligence and EPR/waste models only where the relevant obligations or operational needs apply.
Assign ownership and exchange arrangements.Name the party maintaining each identifier, relationship and resource, and define validation, correction, access, retention and continuity.
Check that the implementation answers the intended request.Verify that a scan retrieves the correct battery information and that records can reproduce the relevant supply, evidence or reporting relationship.

For a portable battery sold in a pack, the combination might be a trade-item model, shared model and batch records, and non-passport QR access. Such a battery does not require an Article 77 passport. For an EV battery installed in a vehicle, the combination includes the host relationship, individual battery identity and passport access; the battery requires a passport under Article 77(1). Neither example determines whether the operator is subject to Chapter VII due diligence: that requires its own Article 47 assessment.

Several matters remain to be resolved before these models become a deployment specification:

Detailed identifier rules remain in Section 12, “Battery and grouping identification”; common QR and passport requirements in Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”; and exchange processes in Section 20, “Supply-chain traceability and information exchange”.
