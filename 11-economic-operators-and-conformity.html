11. Economic operators, conformity and market surveillance ​

This section applies across battery categories, but each duty depends on the requirements applicable to the battery concerned. “Economic operator” includes the manufacturer, authorised representative, importer, distributor and fulfilment service provider, and other persons subject to obligations connected with manufacture, circular operations, making available, placing on the market or putting into service. One organisation may hold several roles. The role must be determined for the particular battery and transaction; a GS1 identifier does not determine it (Article 3(1), point (22)).

11.1 Manufacturer ​

A manufacturer is the person that manufactures a battery, or has one designed or manufactured, and markets it under that person's name or trade mark, or puts it into service for that person's own purposes. Manufacturers are required to (Article 3(1), point (33); Article 38(1)–(8)):

  • ensure compliance with applicable Articles 6–10, 12 and 14;
  • provide clear instructions and safety information in the language or languages determined by the relevant Member State;
  • apply the required markings and labels;
  • draw up technical documentation and carry out, or have carried out, the applicable conformity assessment before placement or putting into service;
  • after conformity is demonstrated, draw up the EU declaration of conformity and affix CE marking;
  • maintain procedures so series-produced batteries remain conforming;
  • provide the required battery identification and manufacturer contact information; and
  • where state-of-health and expected-lifetime requirements apply, provide access to Annex VII parameters through the battery management system.

The referenced requirements retain their category, threshold and date conditions. Further details on product, sustainability and safety requirements are provided in Section 10, “Product, sustainability and safety requirements”, and Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”. Cell and module suppliers must provide manufacturers, free of charge, with information and documentation needed for compliance (Article 39).

11.2 Authorised representative ​

A manufacturer may appoint an authorised representative established in the Union by a written mandate accepted in writing. The mandate may not transfer the manufacturer's product-compliance duties, the obligation to draw up technical documentation or the supply-chain due-diligence duties (Article 3(1), point (63); Article 40(1)–(2)).

At minimum, the representative retains the declaration and specified supporting documents for ten years, supplies conformity evidence following a reasoned authority request and cooperates on action to eliminate risks. It must provide the mandate on request and immediately notify market surveillance authorities where a covered battery presents a risk. Particular tasks may be performed under the manufacturer's responsibility where included in the mandate; permitted tasks vary by module (Article 40(3)–(4); Annex VIII, Part A, point 5; Part B, point 10; Part C, point 6).

11.3 Importer ​

An importer is a person established in the Union that places a battery from a third country on the Union market. It may place only a battery that complies with the applicable product requirements. Before placement, it verifies the conformity assessment, technical documentation and declaration; CE marking and other required markings and labels; accompanying documents, instructions and safety information; and manufacturer identification and contact details (Article 3(1), point (64); Article 41(1)–(5)).

The importer adds its own name or trade mark and contact details to the battery or, where impossible, its packaging or accompanying document. It protects conformity during storage and transport and, where appropriate to risk, sample-tests batteries, investigates complaints, keeps a register where necessary and informs distributors of monitoring. A battery believed non-compliant must not be placed until conformity is restored; if it presents a risk, the importer also notifies the manufacturer and market surveillance authorities.

11.4 Distributor ​

A distributor is a supply-chain person other than the manufacturer or importer that makes a battery available. It acts with due care and, before making a battery available, verifies producer registration, CE marking and other required markings and labels, accompanying material, and manufacturer/importer identity details (Article 3(1), point (65); Article 42(1)–(4)). Further details on producer registration and extended producer responsibility are provided in Section 15, “Producer registration and extended producer responsibility”.

A distributor that believes a battery is non-compliant must not make it available until conformity is restored. If it presents a risk, it informs the manufacturer or importer and market surveillance authorities. Its storage and transport must not jeopardise conformity.

11.5 Fulfilment service provider ​

A fulfilment service provider ensures that warehousing, packaging, addressing and dispatching do not jeopardise compliance with the applicable product requirements. It also cooperates with authorities to eliminate risks and immediately informs market surveillance authorities where a handled battery presents a risk. These duties do not displace those of other operators (Article 43).

11.6 Producer for extended producer responsibility ​

“Producer” is a territory- and transaction-specific EPR role. It can be a manufacturer, importer, distributor or another person that first supplies batteries in a Member State, including incorporated batteries, or sells directly by distance contract to end-users there from another Member State or third country. It is not synonymous with “manufacturer” (Article 3(1), point (47)).

For example, a distributor verifies producer registration, while product conformity is assigned to the manufacturer. A GLN complements organisation records but neither determines this role nor replaces the required registration number (Articles 38, 42(2)(a) and 55). Further details on producer registration and extended producer responsibility are provided in Section 15, “Producer registration and extended producer responsibility”.

11.7 When another actor becomes the manufacturer ​

An importer or distributor is treated as manufacturer if it places or puts a battery into service under its own name or trade mark, modifies an already marketed or commissioned battery so compliance could be affected, or changes its purpose (Article 44).

An operator carrying out preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, repurposing or remanufacturing and then placing the battery or putting it into service is also treated as manufacturer. It must apply adequate quality control and safety to examination, testing, packing and shipment and ensure compliance with relevant Union law, considering any category change. Remanufacturing documentation must be supplied to authorities on request, and an additional Module A assessment is required (Articles 17(3), 38(11) and 45(1)–(2)). Further details on obligations following circular operations are provided in Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”.

Module A is internal production control under manufacturer responsibility. Modules D1 and G involve a notified body: D1 assesses and surveils the production quality system; G verifies the individual unit. Procedure records and correspondence use the official language or languages where the notified body is established, or another language it accepts (Article 17(4)).

The conformity-assessment provisions apply from 18 August 2024, except for the assessment of carbon-footprint and recycled-content requirements, which applies 12 months after first publication of the notified-body list. That conditional date requires current-law verification. Assessment covers applicable requirements only; GS1 standards do not replace it (Article 96(2)(b)).

11.9 Technical documentation ​

Before placement or putting into service, the manufacturer draws up documentation enabling conformity assessment and containing adequate risk analysis. Depending on module and applicable requirements, it includes description and intended use; design and manufacturing material; a specimen of the required label; applied standards and specifications; alternative compliance solutions; calculations and evidence; applicable carbon-footprint and recycled-content studies; and test reports (Article 38(2); Annex VIII, Parts A–C, point 2).

Module D1 additionally requires quality-system controls, records and notified-body surveillance; Module G requires notified-body examination and a certificate. Documentation must be retrievable and supplied to authorities on request (Annex VIII, Part B, points 5–9; Part C, point 4; Articles 38(10), 40(3), 41(7)–(8) and 42(6)).

Identification should associate evidence unambiguously with the covered model or unit. GTIN supports model association; GTIN plus serial number supports unit association where needed. The Regulation does not prescribe these identifiers. Further details on battery identification and identification granularity are provided in Section 12, “Battery and grouping identification”.

11.10 EU declaration of conformity ​

The declaration states that conformity with the applicable product requirements has been demonstrated. It follows Annex IX, includes applicable Annex VIII elements, is kept current, is translated as required by the relevant Member State, and is electronic with paper supplied on request. Where several Union acts require declarations, one declaration covers and identifies them; it may comprise declarations under those acts. By drawing it up, the manufacturer assumes responsibility. Annex VIII generally associates it with a model; Module G associates it with the particular battery (Article 18; Annex VIII, Part A, point 4; Part B, point 7; Part C, point 5).

11.11 CE marking ​

CE marking indicates conformity with applicable Union harmonisation legislation requiring it; it is not a battery, batch or party identifier. It must be visible, legible, indelible and affixed before placement or putting into service. If battery marking is impossible or unwarranted due to its nature, it goes on packaging and accompanying documents (Article 3(1), point (36); Articles 19–20).

Where a notified body is required, its number follows the CE marking and is affixed by the body or, under its instructions, the manufacturer or representative. Physical CE marking is distinct from QR access to the declaration for non-passport batteries; neither replaces the other (Article 13(6)(b)). Further details on labelling, QR codes and battery passports are provided in Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”.

The conformity-assessment modules have different retention triggers. Module A measures from placement of the last battery belonging to the model; Module G uses unit-specific records (Annex VIII, Part A, point 4; Part B, points 3, 7–8; Part C, points 2, 4–5).

The Regulation text has an apparent inconsistency: the manufacturer-retention provision refers to technical documentation in Annex IX, though Annex VIII contains it and Annex IX contains the declaration model. Policies should satisfy the selected module and verify authentic corrigenda and the current consolidated text. The Regulation requires operator and exact-model/quantity traceability, not consumer identity or universal serialisation. A GLN supports party identification, but the complete record must remain available (Article 46).

11.13 Corrective action, withdrawal and recall ​

Manufacturers and importers that believe a battery they placed is non-compliant immediately correct, withdraw or recall it as appropriate. If it presents a risk, they immediately inform the relevant authority and detail non-compliance and action. Distributors ensure appropriate action and provide similar risk notification. Operators supply requested evidence and cooperate with authorities (Articles 38(9)–(10), 40(3), 41(6) and (8), 42(5)–(6) and 43).

Authorities may require correction, withdrawal or recall. Action extends to affected batteries made available by that operator throughout the Union; inadequate action can lead to restriction, prohibition, withdrawal or recall. Measures may also apply where a compliant battery nevertheless presents a risk. Formal defects—including absent or improper CE marking, declaration or documentation, or missing manufacturer/importer details—must be corrected; persistence can lead to restriction, prohibition, withdrawal or recall (Articles 79, 81 and 83).

GS1 identifiers help locate affected parties, models, batches or instances. They do not determine the remedy or prove conformity.

11.14 How GS1 supports party and location identification ​

The Regulation requires party, contact or supply-chain identity information. GS1 enables the capturing and sharing of information by enabling the identification of parties, locations, and functions in a supply-chain using GS1 Location Number GLN and making it possible to register and share using GS1 Verified by GS1. GLN identifies the parties such as manufacturers, authorized representatives, importers, distributors and fulfilment service providers (Articles 38 to 46).

The Global Location Number allocation and management rules are explained in the GS1 GLN Allocation Rules Standard. (https://ref.gs1.org/standards/gln-allocation/)
