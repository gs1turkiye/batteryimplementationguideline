4. Determining the battery category ​

Classify the battery by its design, intended use and weight. Portable batteries are sealed, weigh 5 kg or less, are not specifically designed for industrial use and are neither EV, LMT nor SLI batteries (Article 3(1), point (9)). Portable batteries of general use are the portable subcategory specifically designed for interoperability in the listed formats: 4.5 V (3R12), button cell, D, C, AA, AAA, AAAA, A23 and 9 V (PP3), whether rechargeable or not (Article 3(1), point (10)). Both are covered in Section 5, “Portable batteries”, with additional general-use requirements in Section 5.3. LMT batteries are sealed, weigh 25 kg or less and are specifically designed for traction in wheeled vehicles powered by an electric motor alone or together with human power, excluding EV batteries (Article 3(1), point (11)); see Section 6, “Light means of transport batteries”. SLI batteries are specifically designed for starting, lighting or ignition and can also serve auxiliary or backup purposes in vehicles, other transport or machinery (Article 3(1), point (12)); see Section 7, “Starting, lighting and ignition batteries”. Industrial batteries are specifically designed for industrial uses, intended for such uses after preparation for repurposing or repurposing, or otherwise weigh more than 5 kg and are neither EV, LMT nor SLI batteries (Article 3(1), point (13)); see Section 8, “Industrial batteries”. EV batteries are specifically designed for traction in hybrid or electric vehicles of category L, where the battery weighs more than 25 kg, or of categories M, N or O, without that weight condition (Article 3(1), point (14)); see Section 9, “Electric vehicle batteries”.

Stationary battery energy storage systems are industrial batteries with internal storage designed to store and deliver electricity for the grid or end-users (Article 3(1), point (15)); see Section 8. Button cells are addressed in Section 5, batteries incorporated in products in Section 5.2.1, and circular operations in Section 17, “Reuse, preparation for repurposing, repurposing and remanufacturing”. From 18 February 2027, LMT and EV batteries and industrial batteries above 2 kWh placed on the market or put into service require a passport (Article 77(1)); portable batteries, including those of general use, SLI batteries and industrial batteries at or below 2 kWh instead follow the non-passport QR-information route (Article 13(6)(b), and for SLI batteries also Article 13(6)(c)). If category or industrial-battery capacity is unresolved, passport status remains undetermined. See Section 3.4, “Determine whether a battery passport is required”, and Section 13, “Labelling, QR codes and battery passports”.
